# 20 — The opening: the counter, the first trip, night one, the porch, the landing, the turn

From a new save to the end of Act I. It covers the bible's **P1, P2, 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3** (`01-bible.md` 6.3): the
crews' landing is section 11 and the turn is section 12. The notebook's cutscene is written in `10-notebook.md`, and
this file only hands off to it and picks up after it. The four Act II arcs (`30`–`33`) start from the flags section 12
sets.

Canon order: `00-owner-decisions.md`, then `01-bible.md`, then this file. Voices: `02a-voice-journal.md` (Walt) and
`02b-voice-people.md` (everyone else). Places: `03-places.md`. Loop: `../plans/campaigns.md`. House style is British
spelling. Speech stays as people talk ("Mom", "pop").

**How to read the scripts**
- `[stage]` is a direction for designers and is never shown.
- **If:** is a condition. **Gives:** is what the beat leaves behind: a line on the Walt page, a ring, an item or a flag.
- Player choices are short, as they appear in the talk panel. `(once)` means the choice goes after it's used.
- **The Walt page** is the book's case page (bible 5.3.1). It is written in the player's words, not Walt's. In this
  file it's titled **W.H.** until someone says the name Walt.
- Ids (EP, P, C) are the v3 ids from `03-places.md`. Look each one up again in v9 by name and x/z before building.

## 1. P1 — The counter

### 1.1 Where it sits in the onboarding

taught here), a free map of the home sheet, food for a few days. The story enters at one point only: **when the keeper

so the camp hints teach it the same way.

People in the room: the keeper at the counter and **Josie Hautala** (bible 4.8) behind it or in the back. The store's
visitors for the week are there as usual. **Marlys Lind is never in the room during the opening**, at Moose Lake or

## 2. P2 — The first trip: the tutorial and the story side by side

### 2.1 The rule

The tutorial is the teaching moments in `hints.js`. They teach the first site, the fire, the food hang, the map, the
bottle and the first night. The story never steps into them, and they never mention the story.

1. **On day one, nothing on screen belongs to the story.** The only story thing is the gear itself: the weight of the
   canvas, the old A-frame going up, the BRM-14 on the flap when you open the pack. The day's-beat rule (bible 6.1)
   switches on only after night one.
2. **No story beat teaches a mechanic.** The notebook never says how to pitch, hang or cook. The hints never say
   "Walt", "the notebook" or "the old tent".
3. **Story lines yield.** A story line (a thought, a Walt page line, a party's lore line) waits while any hint moment
   is up or due, the same way hints wait behind a paper. Story lines rank below TEACH.
4. **The notebook doesn't interrupt the evening chores.** It's found when camp is made, but it's read only in bed.
   So the fire, the food hang and the "Dark soon" lines run first, and the cutscene comes when the player lies down.
5. **Systems keep running.** Bears, bugs, wind and rain treat night one like any other night. They never fire during
   the cutscene. A bear the player invited by not hanging the food comes after the cutscene, and wakes them as it
   would anyway. The story doesn't soften the tutorial's consequences.

### 2.2 Where the first trip can go

after night one. Three outfitters also put one of Walt's back-cover carries on the common first trips. Crossing one of

### 2.3 Night one: the find

**The trigger** (bible P2): the first overnight on the first overnight trip on which Walt's bundle is carried. "Carried"
means it is on the player or in their canoe when they make camp. Any shelter counts, or none.

**The find, as camp is made.** It happens on the first of these:
1. **The player pitches the old A-frame.** As the fly unrolls, a brown book slides out of its folds and drops onto the
   tent pad, flat on its back. [It thumps like a book does. No sound cue, no light, no hint.] The dot on it reads
   *Notebook* and the verb is *Pick up*.
2. **The player takes the A-frame off the pack without pitching it** (into another container, or onto the ground). The
   book drops out of the roll the same way.
3. **The player lies down to sleep without doing either.** The cutscene opens on the find itself: the old tent pulled
   out from under the lid, as a pillow or just because it's there, and the book falling out of it. `10-notebook.md`
   writes that frame.

**Picked up.** The notebook goes into the book, always on the player. It is a bound story item from now on (bible
5.3.1, 6.6). Its item card is identity only: *Brown cloth record book. Inside the cover, in ink: W.H. 2010–*. There
is no "why" chip.

**Read before bed.** Until the player lies down, the notebook's verb is *Read*. Using it puts up a thought, once each
evening, and the book closes:

> *In the tent, tonight.*

This is the only time the game holds the notebook back, and it's in the player's own voice. It keeps the chores ahead
of the reading. It doesn't come back after night one.

**If the player sleeps in another shelter** (a tent they bought, a tarp, the open), the find happens by 2 or 3 above,
and the cutscene is the same. The headlamp is the player's.

### 2.4 Trip one's other story moment: "he was here"

After night one, if the player carries one of Walt's back-cover carries (64 / 140 / 186, section 2.2) on this trip, or
camps at one of his starred sites and finds the white stone:
- The book marks it quietly. A carry gets a pencil tick on the sheet beside its rods. A site gets the starred page's
  corner turned.
- The Walt page adds one line under **Places** (section 7): *"64. The first number on his back cover. I carried a 64
  today, Little Trout to Rum."* or *"A white stone on a stump at the landing. He has a page for this site."*
- No hint, no sound, no card.

If the player crossed the carry **earlier on the same trip**, before night one, the mark is added when the back cover
is first read. It applies to that trip only, so old trips aren't trawled.

This is the bible's "the first return then lands on a player who already has a question" (6.3, P2). It is never
required.

## 3. Night one: the hand-off to `10-notebook.md`

**What this file guarantees the cutscene:**
- It starts when the player lies down to sleep on night one, **after** they've set the wake time on the existing sleep
  dial. The dial's choice still decides when they wake.
- The camp is made and the hint queue is empty. Every system event (bear, storm, a party's noise) waits until it ends.
- The player's headlamp is the light, whether or not they own one. If they don't, the cutscene lends it for the length
  of the scene and takes it back.
- Its content is in `10-notebook.md`: page turns, about seven short quiet-years excerpts (N01's header, a weather line,
  a fish line, N02, a bird line, N04's whiskey line, N14's "Slept in the canoe"), then the last page, held. Nothing
  from the watched years (bible 5.3.6).

**What the cutscene hands back:**
- It ends on the last page held, then the lamp goes out, then sleep. The night passes the way the wake dial set it.
- **If the player skips it** (Esc), the night still passes, and at the wake the book is open on the last page. Nothing
  is lost.
- **Flags:** `story.notebook = true`; `story.night1 = { day, site }`; the porch trigger is armed
  (`story.porch1 = 'armed'`); the day's-beat rule switches on for this and every later trip day.
- **The Walt page is created** with the lines in section 7.1. It records every nickname the cutscene showed as
  unmatched, so the pencil has something to work with straight away.

**Requests to `10-notebook.md`** (also listed under New details at the end):

## 4. The morning

[The player wakes in the old A-frame. It's the weather the day has. Light comes through the canvas in a rusty colour.]

**On screen:** nothing the story wrote. The book's tab has the small new-page mark the book always shows for anything
new. If the player hasn't opened the book since the cutscene, `hints.js` gets one new TEACH moment, `notebook`
(once): *"The notebook is in your book: {I}."* That is the only teaching line the story causes, and it teaches the
book, not the story.

**The lid pocket.** The first time after night one that the player opens Walt's pack (to get breakfast out, most
likely), the pack's grid shows its lid pocket. It's an empty slot whose outline is rubbed pale, square, about the size
of a book. [Drawn only. No words.] For years that's where the notebook lived (bible 5.3.1). Opening the pocket, or
hovering it, adds one line to the Walt page:

> *The pack's lid pocket is worn square. The book used to live in there.*

**The notebook in daylight.** All of it can be read now (bible 5.3.1). If the player reads on, the Walt page gathers
nicknames and initials as they appear, unmatched, in a list. Nothing is explained.

**The day's beat.** From this day on the bible's rule holds: at least one small story beat every trip day (6.1). On
trip one's second day it is, in order of preference:
1. one of Walt's back-cover carries or starred sites in reach (section 2.4);
2. reading an entry where he wrote it, if the player is on a nicknamed or starred lake;
3. **a party's lore line** at the next portage or site. One line, from this pool, never twice in a season. None of
   them is a key:

| # | Who says it | Line |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Weekenders, at a portage | "Guy at the outfitter said some old guide used to camp on this lake alone every year. Like, every year. That's commitment." |
| L2 | An older couple at a site | "There was a fella used to come through in October, my dad said. By himself. Never fished where you'd think." |
| L3 | A father, at a landing | "Somebody told us an old guide drowned up on the border a few years back. By himself. Seventy-something." [Beat.] "Wear your life jacket," (to his kid). |
| L4 | A solo, at a shared landing | "Nice pack. They don't make them like that. They make them better, but not like that." |
| L5 | Anglers, from their canoe | "Old-timers used to have their own names for half these lakes. Wouldn't tell you what they were." |
| L6 | A ranger party, passing | "Where'd you come in?" … "Good. That's an old pack." |

## 6. 1.1 — The porch: Tauno stops you

**Tauno Kangas**, 81 (bible 4.1). Boreal crew 1970–79. Grew up three streets from the Haapalas. Paranoid, decent,
ashamed of one thing.

### 6.1 Trigger and staging

**Fires:** the first time the player walks out of any outfitter's door after 5.2 has happened (`story.porch1 =
'armed'`). "Walks out" means through the store door to the porch, the lot or the dock path.

**Staging.**
- [He's leaning on the porch rail, or on the tailgate of an old pickup parked at the edge of the lot: brown, rust
  over the wheel wells, a topper on the back. A seed-company cap, a canvas coat with a union pin on the collar, a
  paper cup from inside. He's looking at the lot, not at the door.]
- **Weather.** In rain he's under the porch roof, and the walk to the truck is quick. Under about 5 °C, or with snow,
  he's in the cab with the engine running, and the scene plays at the driver's window ("Get in. It's cold." The player

- **Daylight only.** Tauno doesn't drive after dark ("My eyes are no good past the junction", `30-mining-years.md`

- **Who else is around.** Store visitors come and go as usual. Once during the scene a visitor comes out of the door

- **The pack.** If the player isn't wearing Walt's pack (it's in the canoe, or set down), his first line changes: "I
  saw you carry that pack in." If it's in the canoe: "That's BRM fourteen in your canoe."

**Walt** with *(W.H.)* in small pencil beside it.

## 9. If the player ignores all of it

The story waits. It never nags, never sets a timer, never puts anything on the map that the player didn't ask for,
and never says "you should". A player who never touches it loses nothing: the bundle is good gear, the notebook is a
good read, and the world goes on.

### 9.1 The rules for re-hooks

- **One re-hook a trip at most**, counting every kind together.
