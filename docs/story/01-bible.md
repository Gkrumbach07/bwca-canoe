# The story bible: Walt's notebook

The single canonical source for every writer and builder of the main story and its side stories. It follows
`00-owner-decisions.md` exactly; where that file is silent, this one decides. If a later file disagrees with this one,
this one wins until the owner says otherwise. Section 10 lists the places where the older drafts must change to match.
Section 11 records what the last revision changed and why.

This is a design document. Short lines in Walt's voice or a character's voice are drafts that fix the content and the
register; the writing passes may reword them, but they must keep the facts. Quote nothing from `00-owner-decisions.md`
into game text.

Read with: `02a-voice-journal.md` (Walt's hand), `02b-voice-people.md` (everyone else), `03-places.md` (the map, ids,
x/z), `../plans/campaigns.md` (the loop: permits, outfitters, board, trip-end screen), `../plans/people.md` (scheduled
parties). Lake, portage and campsite ids are the v3 ids from `03-places.md`; look every one up again in v9 by name and
x/z before it goes into code.

House style: British spelling (colour, neighbours), as in the game's existing text.

## 1. Logline and theme

**Logline.** A stranger borrows an old pack and tent from the back room of a canoe outfitter. On the first night out
they find a dead guide's notebook: ten years of calm solo trips, and a last page written in a hurry. It leads them,
trip by trip and season by season, to papers that two brothers hid in the woods in 1977, to the men who still want them
buried, and to a choice about what those papers are for.

**Theme.** *What a place keeps, and what it costs to carry.* The Boundary Waters look untouched and are not: every lake
holds something somebody left — a drill casing, a lilac at a burned resort, a stone on a stump, a brother. The people of
the edge towns carry their history the same way, quietly and at a price: a crewman who copied numbers to keep his
house, a sister who hides Christmas cards in a coffee can, a friend who sold a phone call to save his son. Walt carried
the papers for forty-two years and never set them down. The game asks the player to pick up what he carried, learn
the country well enough to find it, and decide whether it goes to the light, to the money, or somewhere in between —
knowing that every choice costs someone, and that one of the people it can cost is Walt.
Under that runs the second theme of every good trip report: competence and attention are a kind of love. Walt knew the
wind, so he could fake his own drowning in a way only the people who loved him would read. The player wins the
mystery by learning to read the country the way he did.

## 2. Timeline

All of it is fiction set on real ground and a real calendar. Real public events (the Pioneer closing, the airban, the
1970s copper-nickel hearings, the 1978 Act, Pagami Creek) are texture people allude to; no real person is involved in
any invented event, and no invented character is written into a dated real public scene.

### 2.3 Eino's death (October 1977)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Wed 12 Oct 1977, dawn | Eino leaves from the Little Gabbro road end in his canoe. He tells his mother he is going for ducks on Bald Eagle. His shotgun stays in the porch. He is going alone, early, to fetch the cans from the cellar for the Duluth meeting. Walt is on a shift at the core shed. |
| 12–13 Oct | A front comes through: wind **NW 25, gusts 40**, on the 13th. |
| 13 Oct, evening | Helmi reports him overdue. |
| 14 Oct, morning | A Boreal truck crew finds his canoe swamped against **Bald Eagle's west shore**, below the crews' landing. **No paddle.** His wool jacket is folded on the landing rock. Ostrander waits in Duluth all afternoon. |
| 14–23 Oct | Search: the county's volunteer rescue squad, a Forest Service Beaver, outfitters' boats, dragging and divers. |
| 23 Oct | **Ollie Pekkala**'s drag line brings Eino up from about 40 ft, 300 m off the crews' landing. |
| Nov 1977 | The coroner: drowning. A bruise at the left temple, "consistent with a fall against the gunwale". The **Lake County** sheriff's summary: experienced canoeist, alone, high wind, accidental; canoe recovered "on the west shore". **No foul play indicated.** Inquest closed. |

What did not add up, and who noticed (none of it was ever acted on):
- **The wind.** A NW gale pushes a loose canoe to Bald Eagle's south-east shore. It was found on the west shore,
  **upwind**. Ollie said so to the sheriff's man, the only person who ever said "upwind" aloud. The sheriff said canoes
  do funny things, and the file says only "west shore".
- **The jacket**, folded. Tauno saw it.
- **He swam better than Walt.** Everyone who grew up with them says so.
- **No shotgun** for a duck hunt. Aili knows.
- **The meeting in Duluth** two days later. Ostrander's notes went into a box.

**What happened, as far as the bible lets anyone know.** The call on 3 October went over a shared line, and the men
watching the Little Gabbro road end since the tyres were cut expected Eino to carry the papers to Duluth. When he put
in at dawn on the 12th, they followed him. They meant to frighten him and take the papers; they found nothing in the
canoe, because he had not yet reached the cellar, and it went wrong. That is all. **Who they were is never known.** The
canon is: it is assumed the company sent men or hired some. The papers show Brekke's threat and Sorensen's role; they
never show who was on Bald Eagle that morning. Sorensen died in 2009. Nobody in the game knows, nobody guesses a name,
and no document ever names one. Eino never reached the cellar, so the papers were never found.

**The gamble of the upwind canoe.** Walt staged his drowning the way Eino's was staged, on purpose, as a message for
three people: Aili, Ollie and Tauno. He bet that the men of 1977 were long gone, that Sorensen was dead, and that
Brekke had never heard the wind detail: only Ollie ever said "upwind" aloud, and the 1977 file says "west shore".

**What the company believes.** Wade half-doubts: he read page two. Through 2020 he has a man drive past Aili's motel
and watches her mail, and sees nothing. Brekke prefers "drowned", and says so; when nothing came up after Walt went
in the water, he took the 1977 message for an old man's bluff. That is why the first Christmas card
comes only in December 2020: Walt waited out the watching.

**Walt in 2026** (writers only; never in game): he is 80, lives under the name *Eino Salo* in a camp on a lake north of
Atikokan, and reads the Thunder Bay and Duluth papers online at the library. He does not know who has his notebook.
He stays silent for three reasons. Directions in his hand would prove he is alive, and then the company would lean on
Aili and Jon. He decided in 1977 that exposure costs lives. And he is old and tired: he read that Maggie's records
request went nowhere and believes the fight is lost. What he will not do himself he left to whoever finds his tent:
the last page's "If you are reading this" is for that person. He is never seen, never heard, and never named in game
except as Walt.

## 4. Cast

Ages are in 2026. "Found" uses the outfitters of `campaigns.md`. Each person's voice is two draft lines. Every arc of
section 6.4 ends on a climax scene and a choice; only the mining crew's and the boys' climaxes are spoken confessions
(6.4, "Climaxes differ in form").

### 4.1 The man who stops you

**Tauno Kangas**, 81. *The old man on the porch.*
- **Found:** the first time the player walks out of any outfitter after night one ("Tauno does the rounds of the
  porches all summer"). After that: **Farm 31**'s porch, mornings 7–10, June to October; now and then back at the
  player's home porch.
- **Era:** Boreal crew 1970–79, crew chief from 1974; taconite at Babbitt 1980–2001. Grew up three streets from the

## 5. The clue funnel

The player narrows, in order: **region → lake → shore → landing sign → the way to the camp → the cellar.** Every lock
has at least two independent keys. No key is held by one person only, no lock is behind a person who can be lost, and
nothing is gated by player level.

### 5.1 What is in the cellar

Under a timber lid in the E-7 test pit, two army ammunition cans sealed with tar, wrapped in tar paper. **On top of
them**, where Eino left it on 24 September 1977: his water-sampler's pencil and a scrap torn from a field book, *W. —
Saturday. Bring the good coffee. Eino.* It is the first and only time the player sees Eino's name written, and in his
own hand. **The run begins the moment the player picks it up.**

In the cans:
1. **Four field books** (Aug 1976 – Aug 1977): the crews' Kettle creek readings below the pad, in Eino's, Walt's and
   Tauno's hands. The orange-creek days are there.
2. **The pencilled draft** of the Q3 1976 water report, one corner charred: stations 7–12 crossed through and moved to
   "background", initialled *C.B.*
3. **The green ledger**: Sorensen's cash book, 1975–77. Payments to "L.P." and "H.V."; the last entry is 2 Sep 1977, a
   week before Walt took it.
4. **The carbon**: Brekke to Minneapolis, 30 Aug 1977, *Re: Haapala, E.*
5. A split piece of core from the Kettle pad wrapped in a wool sock. Nobody but Tauno knows what it is.

None of this says who killed Eino. Maggie says so, and so does every ending.

### 5.4 Walt's sign

Since boyhood, as his father did, Walt sets a **white quartz cobble on a stump** (or a flat rock where there is no
stump) at a landing he means to come back to. White quartz is uncommon on the gabbro, so it stands out to someone
looking and to nobody else. **Only the family know it as a sign**: it came from Toivo, and Aili is the only living
person who will say what it means.

It is at his starred sites (all set before June 2018), at the Gull landing, under the Pietro paddle (early June 2018,
the last one before the green Kevlar appeared), and in the camp's floor. From June 2018 he sets no new stones: he
knows he is watched and that a habit can be learned. The two last stones are a goodbye, and he knows the risk: one at
the Birch alders where the hidden canoe lay, and one at the Carp Lake end of Carp Portage in Canada. By 2026 the Birch
stone has tumbled off its stump into the shallows: a white cobble in the water that means nothing unless you know.
Danielle found the Carp stone the next spring. Inside every Christmas card it is the drawing. On the final postcard it
is the only signature.

## 6. Acts, pacing and gates

### 6.1 Shape

The story runs across one season for a focused player, two for most. It never expires and never blocks anything. Each
act is reached by knowledge, not by date; the season gives each part its best weather, not its permission.

| Reference month | Act |
|---|---|
| Late May – June | Prologue, Act I, the crews' landing, the easy nicknames, Mummo's lilacs |
| July | Two of the four groups |
| Early – mid August | The boys at Little Sag (8–15 Aug); the open house; the midpoint search; Maggie |
| Late August – September | The last groups; the drift test; the informant; the warning; the lights, the wolves |
| Mid September – early November | Low water; the far end; the camp; the cellar; the run; the ending |
| If unfinished by freeze-up | Everything carries into 2027. The boys go in again in August; Maggie comes back in May; the watchers stay home in winter |

**Rhythm.** One **big finding** every two or three trips; one set piece per trip from the game's systems (wind, night,
cold, water, a carry); and **a small story beat on most trip days**, wherever the trip goes, but never two days
running when the only beat would be a fallback lore line. Quiet days are allowed and wanted: they make the next beat
land, and they keep the owner's rule that the story is never forced.

**Climax spacing.** No two arc climaxes (6.4) open on consecutive trips. If a second arc's climax becomes available
within one trip of another's, it waits one trip, and the day's beat fills the gap.

**Read it where he wrote it.** Each of Walt's ~40 starred sites and each nickname lake has its notebook entry tied to
the place. Opening the book to that entry while there (the book, E on the page) gives something concrete: the white
stone on the stump, a fish-spot photo match, a second-ink line that only makes sense standing there, or a small Walt
object (a lure, a spoon, a tin, a tobacco tin of matches). These are small findings; none is a lock key.

**The day's beat.** If a game day on a trip has had no story beat by evening, the world may offer one: a scheduled
party at the next portage or site with a line of Walt lore ("Old guide used to camp here alone, they say. Always left
a rock on the stump."), or the book noting a matched entry within reach. No keys. **A fallback lore line fires at
most every other trip day**; on the days between, nothing is offered and the day stays quiet. Once the player has
declined the porch or the door (`20-opening.md` X1/X2), the fallback draws from the non-Walt history pool of
`70-world-texture.md` (logging, the airban, the resort buyouts: its sections 2 and 6.1) instead of Walt lore, so the
country stays storied without pressing. `70-world-texture.md` owns the season-sized pool (its section 9); the L1–L7
lines in `20-opening.md` are for trip one only.

**Beats by outfitter.** At least three per outfitter's reach, so a trip anywhere is a story trip. The lakes are
proposals: writers confirm each is in that outfitter's reach in v9 and pick the site.

**What Wade is doing.** Brekke's orders to Wade are to **let the player lead, then take the papers**: forty years of
looking have not found the cellar, and someone carrying Walt's pack and asking the right questions might. So:
- the watching (tiers 2 and 4) is to learn where the player goes, from a distance; the watchers never follow anyone up
  a carry, because they want the cans lifted, not the ground found;
- **the camp search** is to check whether the player already has the papers (they don't, and the notebook is on the
  player);
- **the warning** (the canoe adrift) is Wade's own idea, not Brekke's: meant to make the player hurry and get careless,
  not to stop them;
- **on the cellar day**, the truck in the lot has the player's car at Little Gabbro or Lake One. A watcher picks them
  up where the company already looks (Bald Eagle in the belt, the Pietro hill, a leaked lake) or, if the player has
  been seen there before, at the two ways into Gull, and hangs back. When the player has not come back out by late
  afternoon, he goes to the last water he saw them on and works on from there; at dusk he is the canoe off the bay
  mouth with no light. He never saw the carry or the camp: he is waiting for the cans to come down to the water.

### 6.3 Prologue and Act I: the gear, the notebook, the porch, the landing

**P1. The counter.** New save; the keeper teaches the permit desk. Rentals are out on every new save; Josie's reason changes with the week (`20-opening.md` 1.2), e.g. "Church group
from the Cities took every pack we own". **Josie** digs out the back-room gear: a canvas pack with a
stencilled patch (**BRM-14**) and an old A-frame tent strapped under its lid, one bundle. Free. Until night one has
happened, Walt's pack and tent cannot be sold or returned.

**P2. The first trip, the first night.** Any route the player picks. On **the first overnight of the first overnight
trip on which Walt's bundle is carried**, whatever the player sleeps in, the notebook slides out of the old tent's
rolled fly as camp is made, and the sleep plays the cutscene (5.3.6). If the player left the bundle at the store, the
trigger waits for the first overnight on which they carry it; Josie nudges once at the counter: "You never took that old
stuff?" The notebook is in the book from then on.

On trip one the player also has a fair chance of crossing one of Walt's back-cover carries: 64 (Little Trout–Rum,
East Bearskin's reach), 140 (Snowbank–Disappointment, Moose Lake's) or 186 (Grandpa–Sea Gull, Round Lake 51's), each
with a starred site and a white stone within a day of it. Crossing one, or reading an entry where he wrote it (6.1),
makes the book quietly mark it: *he was here.* The same mark works for every back-cover number, on that number's own
carry (5.3.1); for "205/50" it is lock 2 key B (5.2). The first return then lands on a player who already has a question.

**1.1 The porch.** The first time the player walks out of any outfitter after night one, the counter there has said
"Before my time", and **Tauno** stops them: "Don't stand there. Walk with me. To the truck. Like we're talking about
bait." He says only a little, and it is all fear and hints:
- "That's BRM fourteen. Walt's crew pack. I'd know that patch anywhere."
- "The outfit he worked for was crooked. Him and his brother had something on them."
- "Walt didn't drown, whatever they told you."
- "His brother went in the water too. Ask me about that after you've stood where they found the canoe."
He tests the player with a small falsehood ("Walt was a Swede, you know"); a player who has read the notebook (sauna,
*löyly*) can correct him, and he warms a step. He will not mark a map ("I don't write on maps"). He gives **one door**:
"Go look at the old crews' landing on Bald Eagle, west shore. Tell me if the crib's still there. Day trip from Little
Gabbro, it's free." Declining is allowed: "Suit yourself. You know where Farm is."

**1.2 The crews' landing.** A day trip: the drive to Little Gabbro (EP 33), a free day permit, Gabbro and the 6 r carry,
then Bald Eagle's west shore at (−25825, 29925). About one session. The player finds the rotten crib, the grade going
south-west into the trees, a 1977 blaze with its dab of orange, and on it a fresh strip of pink flagging that nobody's
survey explains. The west shore is cold and quiet. If the wind is from the north-west that day, the canoe pushes off
the rock on its own. (Tier 2 can start here.)

**1.3 The turn.** Back on a porch, Tauno says **Eino**'s name for the first time, and tells it plainly: the ducks with
no shotgun, the folded jacket, the canoe on the west shore under a nor'wester. "Upwind. Ollie said so. Nobody
listened." That is Act I's turn: a murder. Only then does he name the other doors: "Walt guided out of Moose Lake
longest; ask for Marlys. The fellas he tripped with go up out of Sawbill every August. His sister's at Crane Lake;
she won't talk to you. And come find me at Farm, mornings." The keeper rings Farm, Moose Lake, Sawbill and Little
Vermilion when asked. The easy nicknames (the Front Porch, the Kek, Four Towns, Little Sag), confirmed by any keeper,
teach the pencil.

### 6.4 Act II: the four groups, in any order

Each group is a regional arc in its outfitter's country: a set piece made from the game's systems, at most one photo
favour, small findings along the way, one big finding (its **yield**, a lock key), and a **climax scene** with a
choice whose effect shows in the endings. Keepers pencil every person and place a lead names.

**Climaxes differ in form.** Only two arcs end on a spoken confession: the mining crew (Tauno by the old pad) and the
boys (Denny on the rock), and Denny's must feel unlike anything before it. The guiding arc's climax is **played, not
told**: the player keeps the bunkhouse night with Marlys on the game's clock (the store locked at ten, the lamp in
bunk 3, the hours waited through, the door at two), and she gives a few lines at those moments, never a speech;
`31-guiding-years.md` owns the staging. The family's stays **wordless**: the coffee can, a silence, one line. Around
the climaxes, other people's truths come in **short object-led beats** of a few lines around a thing in hand, not
monologues: Mary over Leo's core-box lid, Len on the dock over the Thursday notebook, Frank's job, Tasha's flag,
Brekke's one conversation. No arc has more than one long speech.

**The mining crew (Farm 31; gentle; Little Gabbro EP 33, South Kawishiwi EP 32).** *Yields the region.*
- *Entry:* Tauno on Farm's porch. Len offers rings at once (true, public, and he reports it: tier 1). Mary at the café.
- *Set piece: the Kettle pad after rain.* After two days of rain, on foot from the crews' landing along the winter-road
  grade, a bushwhack to the west pads, where Tasha's crew is sampling. The player runs the board's Secchi-and-sampling
  task with her at the old pad; the creek below the 1976 trench still runs faintly orange. Mary's favour rides along:
  Leo's initials stamped on a rotten core-box lid at the pad. Bring her the lid. Reward: what the papers are, and "the
  dead-end bay past Bald Eagle … the far end" (lake key).
- *Yield:* Tauno's 1976 crew map (region; E-7's false position and mirrored sketch). Decodes Two Stump and V.'s water.
  Asked later about E-7, he describes an old pit, the orange-dab blazes and core spill.
- *Climax: the clean book.* Tauno asks the player to drive him to the Little Gabbro road end, where the crew camp was.
  Standing by the old pad, he confesses: he recopied three days of readings for Brekke in 1976; he saw the jacket; he
  saw Walt take the ledger; he carried Walt's message to Sorensen. **The choice:** ask him to go on record when the
  time comes, or don't. It decides whether the journalist ending has his testimony (7.1). Asking costs nothing now; he
  says "If it comes to that. If."
- *Small findings:* drill casings in the moss along the grade; the crib dock; the open house notice on Farm's board in
  August, with free coffee and Brekke shaking hands.

**The guiding years (Moose Lake 25; gentle, busy).** *Yields the lake, and the hinge.*
- *Entry:* Marlys at the counter on her Tuesdays and Wednesdays ("I don't remember much"). She knows the tent the first
  time the player carries it in. Hap on the porch at weekends.
- *Set piece: Hap's tour from memory.* Hap can't go out any more, so he describes his and Walt's 1980s guide day out of
  Moose Lake, place by place, and the player paddles it and tells him what's there now (by conversation). One of his
  places is wrong: he puts the old tow dock on the wrong shore of Prairie. A player who gently sets him right earns the
  real talk: Walt's October trips, Little Gabbro, the far end, and "he never once said Pietro" (lake key). Decodes the
  Front Porch, the Kitchen and the Long Room.
- *Marlys's favour:* find the 2019 bunkhouse register and the message pad in the boathouse loft (a walk-in search in
  the store's own buildings). Reward: *W. Helstrom, day use, parked*, and the pad's page with the Duluth number.
- *The drift test (the hinge between Acts II and III).* Sheila, at the landing, gives page two. On a night with a S–SSE
  wind she runs the player and canoe up Moose, Newfound and Sucker in the rescue boat, as far as the Sucker–Birch
  bypass, and waits there. From there the player is alone on Birch in the dark: paddle to mid-lake, let go, and watch
  the canoe go north. The Walt page's line changes to "went north" (5.6). Sheila asks nothing; on the ride back she
  says only, "Page two."
- *Yield:* the lake (Hap), with the register as the company's first fingerprint on 2019.
- *Climax: the night.* With the register in hand, Marlys keeps the night with the player in bunk 3, on the clock, and
  the rest comes in short lines at its hours: the call she carried, the truck at one, the door at two, the box at six,
  and her lie to Wade. Played, not told (above). **The choice:** bring Sheila in (Marlys tells her story to a deputy
  who will write it down), or let it lie. It decides whether the county "reviews" 2019 in the journalist ending (7.1).
  If the player brings Sheila in, she adds, "We overlapped two years, Wade and me."

**The boys (Sawbill 38; gentle).** *Yield the shore, and hide the informant.*
- *Entry:* Carol at the café, any time; Bob, Frank and Denny at the store in early August or at Little Sag
  9–14 August (their camp is a scheduled party; the player may visit, eat, and be made to buy the first round, "Walt's

- *Photo favour, Carol:* see whether Gene's paddle is still at Frost ("Gene's", C1125) and photograph it. Reward:
  Gene's 1998 slide (shore key) and, on a later visit, the nicknames as a story.
- *Set piece: the laker bet* on Little Sag, on Walt's old spoon; the biggest fish buys the round. Reward: the 1998 trip
  from Bob and Frank: Gull, the east bay, the beaver dam, wet knees, "the fishing was not lousy", and "Denny missed that

- *Denny* over-helps throughout (rings the Isabella, calls the outfitter, drives over, leaves his number). His three
  tells come one at a time (4.4).
- *Frank's* Northmark job and the obvious reading of "Sparky" make him the suspect. The player can clear him (the canary

- *Yield:* the shore.
- *Climax: the last night at Little Sag.* The informant reveal (5.7), on Walt's sitting rock, with its choice.

**The family (Little Vermilion 12; remote, Quetico's door).** *Yields the landing sign; hides that Walt lived.*
- *Entry:* Aili at the motel counter: coffee, bars, nothing. "You have his tent. Is it holding up?" (Bring it; she
  touches the fly.) Jon on the dock, hostile.
- *Photo favour, Aili:* Mummo's lilacs (S7), a June photo of the ruin; or June's coffee pot (S9). Either opens her.
- *Set piece: Sand Point.* On a trip out of Crane Lake, a boat trails the player across Sand Point toward the border.

- *Yield:* the landing sign. *Small:* the bootleggers' story (S6 starts here); Gord on Thursdays.

**Tauno's call.** On the first porch visit after the player's **second big finding**, Tauno asks: "There's a woman

**The midpoint.** On the first trip that begins after **two big findings**, **tier 2** and Tauno's call:
- **The camp search.** The player comes back from fishing or a walk to find the tent opened, the packs gone through,

  was on the player. (N36 has told them what the fold means.) At the next counter Wade is buying bait: "Lot of country
  up here. Easy to get turned around."
- **Maggie's note** at the home counter, via Josie: "leech order, three dozen, Hog Creek, ask for Maggie." At Hog Creek
  she lays out the stakes (the baseline), the state's E-7 list (lake key), Ostrander's note, and Walt's 2019 letter. The
  handwriting matches the notebook. She is willing to wait for the papers; waiting is allowed **until the cellar is
  lifted**, never after.

### 6.5 Act III: in too deep

- **3.1 Watched.** The green Kevlar on the player's lakes; boot prints at their landings; Dan's offer, made kindly
  ("for the history"): **$1,000** for anything from the old days. Dan buys real junk only (4.6).
  - **Brekke.** One designed conversation, at Northmark's open house at Farm 31 in August, or at any Thursday lunch
    with Len from tier 2 through October. He speaks warmly of Walt and calls Eino a hothead. He shows what Len has
    told him ("Len says you've been asking about Bald Eagle. Lovely lake. Cold."). He asks nothing directly. Then:
    "If you ever find what that family thought they had, you'll find it's old paper. Old paper burns." Three answers:
    - *Lie* ("Just here for the fishing."): nothing changes.
    - *Deflect* ("Walt seemed to think it mattered."): Len's next Thursday report says the player is careful.
    - *Needle him* ("Stations seven to twelve.", if Maggie or Tauno has given the numbers): his ring hand goes still;
      he says "I wouldn't know." The watchers step up a tier at once, and his line in the money ending changes (7.2).
  - **Wade at a put-in.** Once in tier 3, at a landing, standing by his truck in clean boots: "That old tent of Walt's
    holding up?" Nothing else. He knows the gear because he went through it in 2019.
- **3.2 The informant** (5.7), whenever the player has built it; the boys' arc's climax.
- **3.3 The warning.** Once, on a calm September night at a site near shore, after tier 3: the player wakes at first
  light to find the canoe thirty metres off, drifting, **paddle gone**. They swim for it (or use the spare, if they have
  Walt's from Pietro). The paddle turns up on the far shore of the bay. Nothing is said, by anyone. It is the echo
  pointed at the player, and it is Wade's idea.
- **3.4 Converging.** The wrong hill on Pietro; the rods; the far end pencilled and inked; the east bay; the white
  stone. If the player arrives before mid-September, the summer landing needs the stone (they must be looking); after,
  the low water shows the step and the rod.
- **3.5 The carry and the camp.** About 880 m, 175 rods, never drawn on any sheet: up the dam's back, along the east
  bank of the flowage (pole, wade or carry), then a climb east through fifteen years of jack pine to the knob and its

  the 1976 photo of two young men at a fly camp, a bark-and-moss roof under the cedar. **Untouched since October 2017.**

- **3.6 The cellar.** Core spill up the ridge for those who can read it; 620 m toward the midwinter sunrise; a square

  lifts. Eino's pencil and note on top; two cans, tar, paper beneath. Picking up the note starts the run. **There is

Camping at the camp is off a designated site, as anywhere; a ranger would fine it. The game neither forbids nor
encourages it.

### 6.6 The run

**Bound story items.** The notebook and the two cans:
- cannot be sold, given to a keeper, or offered at any counter;
- are never lost in a capsize or swamping (the notebook rides in the book; the cans are lashed in like the spare
  paddle) and are never touched by a bear;
- if set down, the dot offers only *Pick up*; walking more than about 50 m away from them is refused with one short
  thought ("Not leaving those.").
During the run, an evacuation, a swamping or a hypothermia event resets the player to the last checkpoint with the
cans in hand, exactly like being caught.

**The meeting point.** Maggie names two; the player picks with a leech-order note (the slip names the place; the number of dozens is the days from today):
1. **Lake One, EP 30** (Moose Lake's reach): Walt's own plan, "back door to the Front Porch in one dark". The designed
   run.
2. **Little Gabbro, EP 33**: fast, over Bald Eagle's open water. The twist: the lot is full of trucks, and Maggie's note
   under the permit box moves the meeting to **Farm 31** the next dawn, down the South Kawishiwi. A second night,
   resting tied off.

If no meeting is set when the cellar comes up, the run's goal is any entry point an outfitter serves; at dawn the
keeper's phone there brings Maggie (if met; if not, the keeper rings the Ledger).

**The start.** Coming down the carry at dusk, a canoe sits off the bay mouth with no light. A radio clicks over the
water. The run state begins.

**The rules of the run** (all from existing systems; teach once, at the moment of need):
- **No campsites.** The tent verbs don't appear. Rest is only **in the canoe, tied off** to a snag or in reeds. The
  tie-off verb is part of the main build: N14 teaches it the first time the player tries to rest during the run (or
  earlier, if S2 came first).
- **Light.** Headlamp white is seen at long range over water, red at short range (S3's red lens), off at none, but off
  is dark. Watchers sweep with lights at the portage landings.
- **Sound.** Wind over 15 covers paddling; a dropped pack on rock carries.
- **Cover.** Reeds, shore shadow, the far side of an island, under a turned canoe.
- **The scanner.** Walt's dry bag at the Camdre end of P24 holds a handheld scanner: with it the player hears the
  watchers by lake names ("He's on Pietro. Going north."), Wade's voice and two others.
- **Walt's stashes** on the north route: the spare paddle under the white stone at the Pietro end of P530; the dry
  bag at the Camdre end of P24; the Grumman upside down in the alders at the Rock Island end of P26 (hide under it while
  a light goes by). The set piece is **Rock Island's 214 rods in the dark**.
- **Checkpoints:** the pit at dusk, **every stash, every portage end**, and every tied-off rest. A catch costs at most
  about 15 real minutes.
- **Length.** Pietro to Lake One is about 9 game hours (Walt's own time, N29); Gull to Lake One about 10, roughly 75
  real minutes.

**Caught.** A watcher's light holds the player within about 25 m, or they are met at a landing. A light in the face;
Wade's line, "Evening. Long way from a site, aren't you.", either from the landing where he stands on shore in clean
boots, or over the scanner while the light holds. Fade. **Nothing is shown.** The player is back at the last
checkpoint, cans in hand, same night, with the watchers reshuffled. It is never an ending and never costs anything but
time.

**The last crossing.** Lake One at first light. A watcher canoe sits off the EP 30 landing. The player gets past it by
the dawn fog bank (October mornings), by a wind over 15, or by landing at a far site and walking the last stretch of
shore, and comes up into the lot.

**Freeze-up.** The pit's verb is closed from 15 November to ice-out (N41). If the season ends during a run anyway, the
season ends at the last checkpoint and the run resumes there at ice-out, with the watchers and Maggie back.

**Arrival.** The dawn lot (7.0). The choice is made there.

## 7. Endings

There are two real choices, and each one costs someone. **There is no "caught" ending, no "put it back" ending and
no way to hold the cans without choosing.** Before the cellar is lifted the player may take as long as they like; once
it is lifted, the run ends in one scene, and the scene ends only when they choose.

### 7.0 The dawn lot

The meeting point at first light. Maggie's grey wagon at one end of the lot. At the other, Dan's truck: Wade told him
where, and Dan has come himself, sincerely pleased, with Brekke in the passenger seat. Wade's truck idles by the permit
box. A party is loading a canoe at the landing, which is why nobody does anything but wait.

The player may sit on the bench and Rest, but cannot leave the lot with the cans ("Not with these."). They walk to
one of the two vehicles:
- **To Maggie** with the cans: the journalist ending (7.1).
- **To Dan** with the cans: the company's money (7.2). Dan names the offer. If the Walt page says "went north", Wade
  walks over and adds the one thing that makes it a dilemma: "And nobody goes looking for the old fella any more.
  Whatever's in Canada stays in Canada." The player can still turn back until the cans change hands.

The ending choices also gather what the arcs decided: Tauno on record or not, Sheila brought in or not, Denny turned,
told on or left, Aili told "I know" or not, Brekke needled or not.

### 7.1 The journalist (the papers go to Maggie)

## 9. Rules of the story

**Never revealed.** Who killed Eino. No document, person or ending names or guesses a killer. The papers prove the
threat and the bribes, not the killing.

**Never shown.** Walt after 1998. No figure, no voice, no meeting, no photo later than Gene's slide. His Canadian name
and his lake are for writers only. The postcard is all.

**Never possible.** A "caught, you lose" ending; a "put it back" ending; holding the cans without choosing; a fail
screen for leaving the path; a lock on player level; a story deadline the player can miss; losing, selling or
abandoning a bound story item (6.6).

**Tone.**
- A calm canoe game with a mystery under it. It waits; it never pushes. A player who ignores it loses nothing.
- Menace by presence and politeness: a truck with its lights off, a neat fold, a canoe adrift, a man asking after your
  tent. No threats in plain words, no weapons drawn, no violence on screen. The run's worst moment is a light in the
  face and a pleasant voice.
- Understatement everywhere (`02b-voice-people.md`). The biggest things get the smallest words.
- One set piece per trip, made from the game's systems; at most one photo favour per arc.
- Humour stays alive the whole way: the boys, the Squatch, the bootleggers' box, Walt's knees.

**Ethics.**
- Every person, company and crime is fictional. No real private person's name; real public figures only as offstage
  voices; real outfitters, resorts and businesses never named. No invented character is placed in a dated real public
  scene (the 1977 hearing is one undated line of memory).
- The fraud is in Boreal's own permit filings, never in any real public study; the Kettle pad, the Kettle creek and the
  Kettle project sit on no real deposit or lease, and no real creek name is put next to "naturally mineralised" on
  screen.
- Mining is even-handed in structure: the villains are particular men; decent people (Dan, Tasha, Frank) want the
  mine, decent people (Bob, Tauno) don't, and neither side owns the crime. **No ending decides the mine:** the
  journalist ending brings down the men and leaves the project to a fair, honest fight; the money ending builds it on a
  lie, and the people on its own side say so.
- No Ojibwe sacred material, no spirit beings, no Wendigo, no pictograph meanings, and no pictograph site used as story
  content (there is no S10). Ojibwe characters are people with jobs and histories (Tasha); no real band is named as
  theirs, and none is used to voice a band's position on mining.
- Real tragedies by shape only. The plane in S5 has no death. Eino's and Walt's "drownings" are fiction on a real
  calendar.
- The border Birch Lake is never tied to mining.
