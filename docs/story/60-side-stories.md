# Side stories from Walt's notebook

The side stories of the bible's section 8, designed in full: nine of them, S1–S9, and S10 left blank on purpose (North
Hegman is a place, not a story; see S10). Each has a notebook hook in Walt's hand (quoted verbatim from
`10-notebook.md`, the only text of the notebook), a real place, a person, a set piece made from the game's own
systems, conversations, a reward, red herrings, a version for a player who never opens the notebook, and a window that
ends the way the world would.

Canon order: `00-owner-decisions.md`, then `01-bible.md`, then this file. Voices: `02a-voice-journal.md` (Walt),
`02b-voice-people.md` (everyone else). Places and ids: `03-places.md` (v3 ids; look each up again in v9 by name and
x/z before it goes into code). This file hands off to the arc files where a scene belongs to them, and says so:
- Aili's two hand-ins (the lilac photo, June's pot) are written in `33-family.md` 6.4. S7 and S9 own the place and the
  finding; 33 owns her answer.
- Gus's board notice for the lilacs is `31-guiding-years.md` GY-T6. S7 writes its text and the person who posted it.
- Sheila's lines about Ollie are `31-guiding-years.md` GY-09b. Tauno's lead to Ollie is `30-mining-years.md`.
- The keepers' names, ambient lines and the world's rumours about each side story are `70-world-texture.md` (6.1, 8).
  This file adds only the lines its own people say.
- How the run uses the tie-off rest, the red lens, tracks and old clearings is `50-finale.md` (3, 6).

**Notation.** S1–S9 here are the bible's side stories (S10 is a deliberate blank). `70-world-texture.md` uses `S0`–`S7` (in code font) for
main-story stages; this file writes those as "stage `S3`+". Condition keys follow 70 section 0 (`knows:`, `month:`,

directions; *Gives:* says what a branch hands over. Finished game text is in quotation marks or in code blocks.
House style is British spelling, in dialogue too.

### 0.2 How one starts

Every side story has **three doors**, and any one of them starts it:
1. **Its person or its notice.** A notice on a board, a person at a counter or on the water. No notebook needed.
2. **A rumour** that names the place or the person (70 6.1 holds the world's versions).
3. **Walt's entry**, read anywhere: a head start (where, when, what to look for). Read **at the place** ("read it where
   he wrote it", bible 6.1), it also gives the small finding each section names.

A side story is **started** when the player takes its first real step: meets its person past the opener, takes its
notice, or does the thing at its place (sleeps at the ridge site, howls at a station, opens a pit). Reading an entry
alone starts nothing.

**Slots.** At most **three** of S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6 and S8 are open at once (the campaigns' 2–3 rule; the main story
never counts). S7 and S9 take no slot: they are places that wait, and nobody is waiting on the player. With three
open, a new person says a "later" line and asks again next visit:
- OLLIE: "Evening." *[He doesn't stop. Next walk evening he does.]*
- DUANE: "You look busy. I'll be here. I'm always here."
- ELAINE: "Come back when you've got a night to waste. That's what this takes."
- KARI: "You've got your hands full. I'm here till October."
- PETE: "Go do your thing. The plane's not going anywhere. That's the problem."
- ORRIN: "Some other time. Old things keep."
- TOM: "Where'd you come in?" … "Good camp." *[He mentions the sled next time.]*

A notice stays on the board until its window closes; a person stays until theirs does. Nothing is lost by waiting
except the window.

### 0.3 The page in the book

Each started side story gets **one journal page in the player's hand**, the same hand as the Walt page. It holds what
the player knows: people, places, times, photos pinned in, what was said, and at the end how it ended. It never holds a
next step. Show only what's missing: a page with a gap reads like a page with a gap.

Example, S1, after the third meeting:

The man on the Stuart.

When a side story ends, its page gets one last line in the same hand ("Ollie's back in June." / "The sled went to the
yard in town." / "Kari left. She got most of it.").

The trip-end screen stamps one **first** per side story (each section names it) and lists "story beats found this
trip" as it does for Walt's pages.

### 0.5 Side stories and the main story

- **No lock is held only by a side story.** Every main-story reward here has another key (bible 5.2). A player who
  never plays a side story finishes the main story.
- **Side-story places are not watched**, unless the player names one to a leak in the canary trap (bible 5.7). Then the
  green Kevlar can turn up there like anywhere else (the sixth light in S3; two men with no rods on the Kek in S5).
- **No watcher steps into a side story's set piece.** The night on Big Moose is animals. The light on Fourtown is not
  about the player. The menace belongs to the main story.
- **During the run** (stage `S6`) no side story moves. Its people wait. What the player has already learned works.
- **After the endings** the side stories carry on (section 11).
- **The day's beat.** A side story's place in reach counts as the day's story beat (bible 6.1).

## S1. The man on the portage

### At a glance

- **Hook:** N12, August 2013 (quiet years).
- **Place:** the **Stuart Portage**, 563 r, 2,833 m (a world-fixes entry): from the Echo Trail road end at Stuart EP 19
  (−57783, −196) north to the Stuart River (−57693, −2811). Long, wet in the middle, a hill near the river end.
  Moose River North 16's reach (keeper Leon Zupancic).
- **Window:** Ollie walks **Tuesdays and Thursdays**, from the first Tuesday of June to the last Thursday of September.
- **Trips:** two or three. All of it fits free day trips from EP 19 (pay for the drive only), but by September the
  walk ends after dark, and a day permit out after dark is a ranger's fine. An overnight permit with a night on the
  Stuart River avoids it.
- **Person:** Ollie Pekkala, 79.
- **First stamped:** *Beat the squad on the Stuart* (or *Carried the Stuart for Ollie*).

### The hook

NB30 in `10-notebook.md`, verbatim (the notebook's text is the only text; this file quotes it):

```
Aug 13 — EP 19 — solo, 6 days food.

On at 3, which was foolish. P 563r. Hot. Deer flies the size
of sparrows. 2x, so call it 1,689r walked. Didn't do the sum
till the end, which was wise. Voi voi.

Man on the 563r going the other way at dusk, no pack. Said
"Evening."

Aug 15. Back over the 563r, 1x this time, out of food and
pride. Same man at dusk. Same word.

[O. Took me till the third time. He knew me the first.]
```

The second-ink line is the only second ink in S1. It reads as nothing to anyone who doesn't know who O. is. The entry
never names a weekday: in Walt's code "Thursday" only ever means Len (10, New details 1). 13 and 15 August 2013 were a
Tuesday and a Thursday, Ollie's two walk evenings, and nothing on the page says so.

### The place

The road end has a small lot, a Forest Service sign and a register box. The trail runs north through spruce and alder,
dips into a long boardwalked wet stretch in the middle, then climbs a rocky hill and drops to a landing rock on the
Stuart River. Ollie sits on that rock for ten minutes each walk evening and looks at the river.

**Reading N12 on the Stuart** (the small finding): the book marks the page *he was here*. Nothing else. The man is the
finding.

### The person

**Ollie Pekkala**, 79 (bible 4.9). Rescue squad 1970–2008, 38 years; found Eino on 23 October 1977 with his own drag
line. Taught Sheila Novak the drag line in 1991 (31 GY-09b). His wife **Doris** died in the winter of 2011; the doctor
told him to walk; he has walked the Stuart twice a week since the spring of 2012. Lives in Ely. An old pickup with a
faded squad decal in the back window. Wears a squad jacket that doesn't close any more.

*Schedule:* parks at the EP 19 lot at 18:50, walks north at 19:00, sits on the river landing rock about 19:40–19:50,
walks back, in the lot by about 20:35. He walks at an old man's steady pace and never stops on the trail.

*Voice:* one word, then very few. Practical. Weather, water, the trail. He never says "Eino": "the young one", "the
brother", "the Haapala boy". He says "upwind" once.

### How it starts

- **The person:** pass him on the Stuart on a walk evening.
- **Rumour:** 70 6.1 ("Fella on the Stuart Portage at dusk…"); Leon at Moose River North (70 8.6, once the player has
  started S1, or asked about the Stuart); Tauno ("Ollie Pekkala. Drag line, nine days in…", 30); Sheila (31 GY-09b).
  Any of these rings the Stuart Portage if it isn't on a sheet the player owns.
- **The board:** a Portage survey task for the Stuart Portage (an existing kind) at Moose River North puts players on
  the trail. Leon, handing it over in a walk-evening week: "If you're on it after supper, you'll meet Ollie. Say
  'evening' back. That's the whole conversation, the first couple of times."
- **Walt's entry:** N12 gives the portage and the time of day.

### The steps

1. **First meeting.** On the trail or at a landing, a walk evening. OLLIE: "Evening." He walks on. E on him gets
   "Evening." again, over his shoulder.
2. **Second meeting.** The same. One evening can give two meetings (out and back) if the player waits at an end.
3. **Third meeting.** He stops (conversation A).
4. **The loaded carry**, on any walk evening after (the set piece).
5. **At the river end afterwards** (conversation B). The log page.
6. From then on, **"Evening."** and a second line, through September (conversation C).

### The set piece: the loaded carry

Ollie stands at the road end with a watch on a leather strap. The player has the canoe and at least one pack.
- The time runs from the moment the player leaves the road-end landing with the canoe up and a pack on, to the moment
  the canoe comes down at the river-end landing. **One trip:** canoe and pack together (the game's existing single
  carry). A set-down to rest is allowed and costs its minutes.
- **No timer on screen.** The player knows the time as Ollie does: by the clock. The game reads the two moments.
- The trail does the work: the carry pace, the load's weight, energy, the wet middle (slow, sucking mud between the
  boardwalk sections), the hill. In September the light goes during the carry, and the headlamp comes on.
- **The squad record is 49 minutes** (1981). *[Builders: tune the number so a light single load at full energy beats
  it by a minute or two and one set-down loses it, then change Ollie's line to match. At the game's carry pace the
  2,833 m take about 47 minutes.]*
- Ollie starts walking out a few minutes after the player and arrives at the river end about 45 minutes after the
  start. He reads the result off the player's face and then off his watch.

**Results.**
- **Beat it:** OLLIE: "Forty-seven." *[He looks at the watch again.]* "Well. My knees just retired."
- **Missed, first try:** OLLIE: "Fifty-three. Not bad. Not forty-nine." … "Tuesday." *[Or Thursday: the next walk
  evening.]*
- **Missed, second try:** OLLIE: "You carried it twice. That's what I wanted to see. Forty-nine was a younger back than
  either of ours." *[Counts as done.]*
- **Gave up partway** (canoe down and left, or the player walks back): OLLIE: "Happens. The hill gets everybody."
  *[Counts as a try.]*

Either way, conversation B follows.

### Red herrings

- **A man alone on a remote portage at dusk, no pack, one word.** In tier 2+ he reads as a watcher. He isn't: the
  watchers never walk a carry (bible 6.2), and his word is the same as Wade's on the run on purpose (40), so the run's
  "Evening." lands on a player who has learned to like it.
- **N12's flat repetition** ("Same man at dusk. Same word.") reads like Walt counting watchers. It is 2013, the quiet
  years. A player who knows the three stretches can tell.
- **"I copied everything that fall."** There is no box. One page.

### Without the main story

A friendship that starts with one word, a time trial on the longest carry you can drive to, and a page of local
history about a Finn kid on Bald Eagle in '77. If the player later starts the main story, Tauno's turn (1.3) lands on a
story they have half-heard, and the log page gains its Walt-page line then.

### The window and how it ends without you

He stops walking after the last Thursday of September, "when the leaves are down", and is back the first Tuesday of
June. Nothing is lost: a player who never did the carry can do it next summer. The page's last line: *"Ollie's back in
June."*

### Build notes

Ollie is a scheduled person on the Stuart's polyline (actors.js walk, `people.md`). The timed carry needs no new
system: two clock readings. The day-permit fine after dark is the existing rule.

### The story (writers)

### The place

**Reading N03 at the beach rock** (the small finding): on a calm, bright day the water off the rock reads a shade
clearer for the player, and a dark shape shows on the sand at the very edge of seeing: where to cast.

### How it starts

  Alice beach site. Every kid who camps there's lost a lure on it. Half the jigs in Alice are in that pot."

- **Aili** (33 6.3): "The blue one. He lost it off a rock somewhere."
- **Walt's entry:** N03 (Coffee Lake: finding the pot inks the nickname, bible 5.3.4).

### The steps

1. Hear of it, or read N03.
2. At Alice: stand on the beach rock (or, **if a party holds the site**, sit in the canoe just off it: one party per
   site, `people.md`, but the water is anybody's).
3. Cast toward the 8 ft sand, 10–15 m out, with anything that runs on the bottom (a jig is best), and work it back
   slowly along the sand.
4. It snags on something heavy. Work it free (the set piece).
5. Keep the pot, or take it to Aili (33).

### The set piece: a slow, dead weight

Fishing on foot or from the canoe, the game's own cast and retrieve. A bottom lure dragged through the pot's couple of
metres catches its bail. A lure that runs high (a spinner, a crankbait) goes over it. Each pass through the spot
catches about half the time; on a calm, bright day the dark shape shows exactly where to drag. Blind, it's a few
casts, and the kid's "half the jigs in Alice" is the hint.

**The hook-up** reads at first like any bite: the rod loads and the tension bar comes up. Then it doesn't behave like
a fish. **The existing fight, played slow and dead:** no run, no head shake, no line taken. It gives a little at a
time as the reel works and the sand lets go, then sticks, then gives. Reel too hard against it and the bar goes red
the same as with any fish: the line breaks, the lure is lost, and the pot stays where it is for the next cast. Easing
off and reeling again is the whole skill. It takes about a game minute.

<!-- id: S9.pot.dot quoted -->
**It comes up by its bail handle**, the jig hooked through the wire, streaming sand and water, the lid still on. Into
the hand, not the stringer. The dot on it: *"Blue enamel coffee pot. A chip on the lip."* Sand pours out of the spout
for a while.

<!-- id: S9.kid.3 each -->

  Dad, it's a pot." / PARENT: "I see that." / KID: *[gives it back]* "You can keep it. I just wanted to know."
