## 1. The emotional core

Walt had a family he couldn't talk to and a brother he couldn't mention. He had a town that thought he was strange.
And he had the boys: four Duluth church men who hired him in 1985 and then simply kept coming. Over twenty-six
Augusts the trip turned into the one place where Walt was only a cook with bad knees and good coffee. The boys don't
know what he carried. They knew him anyway, and better than anyone.

The arc is about what friendship is made of and what it can survive. Three old men are keeping a trip alive with one
man fewer every few years: Gene's heart in 2016, Walt's canoe in 2019. The fourth is the one who broke it. Denny
sold his best friend's whereabouts to save his own son, and Matt lived. For seven years Denny has believed that the
call drowned Walt. Under all of it is something the player can learn and Denny can't bear to hear. Walt knew which of
them it was by that August. He still picked up the phone on the 17th, still said "sure, Sparky", and then fed Sparky
one last useful lie and went out the door.

The player doesn't decide whether Denny is guilty. He is, and he says so. The player decides what a man who did a
terrible thing for love is owed: a use, a reckoning or a silence. The boys' August trip carries the answer into every
ending.

The rhythm the arc works to: **loud, loving and funny on top, one quiet man underneath.** It should be the warmest
company in the game right up to the night on the rock.

### 2.2 The rituals (use them; they are the arc's furniture)

- **Walt's rule.** The newcomer buys the first round. At the Sawbill store that means coffee and bars for the table, paid
  at Carol's counter. At camp it means something shared from your own pack. The boys teach it once, the first time.
- **Walt cooked.** Nobody else was allowed near the pan. Bob still carries Walt's black steel pan in the food pack
  every year. Nobody has cooked in it since 2011.
- **The sitting rock.** At C1210, a flat granite shelf at the point. Walt sat there alone on the last night of every
  trip and they left him be. Since 2020 his cup sits there on the last night instead.
- **The 473.** Always "the 473" or "the long one", cursed both ways. Bob: "I've measured it with my knees."
- **The 15th.** Out at the Sawbill landing, then up to Carol's counter for the round, which the laker bet's winner pays. (Walt's other
  rule: "Biggest fish buys. A man that lucky can afford it.")
- **The oath.** "Swear on the coffee." It is a running joke about the lake with lousy fishing. Bob still won't say its
  name, and Frank pretends he's about to.
- **Booking.** Bob books the Sawbill permit the morning reservations open. He has never once needed to. He does it
  anyway.

## 3. The people

### 3.1 Bob Tervo ("Teach"), 74

Retired high-school shop teacher. He lived in Duluth and moved to Tofte in 2012. He has an anti-mine sign in the yard
and never mentions it. A patient man who explains things.
- **Found:** the Sawbill store (Sawbill 38), **Tuesdays and Saturdays** 9:00–14:00, June–October. Every day 1–7 August (packing). On
  the trip (8–15 Aug).
- **Wants:** to keep the trip going. **Fears:** that it dies with them. He does the arithmetic of their ages and won't
  say it.
- **Secret:** Walt's words at the Sawbill landing, 15 August 2019. He has never asked which one, on purpose.
- **Lies about:** nothing. He vouches for Denny's alibi, because the alibi is true.
- **Trust:** opens when the player has bought the first round, done Carol's favour, or fished the laker bet with them.
  Any one of the three is enough.
- **Gives:** the 1998 story with Frank (key 3A). "Denny missed that one. Kid was sick." Matt's treatment "got paid for
  somehow" (**hard**). Walt's 2019 line. Clearing Frank, once the player has tested Frank or knows the nicknames (5.8):
  "Frank guards their parking lot nights. He'd die before he'd talk."
- **Voice:** full sentences, a teacher's patience, practical first. "Well." to begin a hard thing. Never swears.
  > "Walt cooked. Walt always cooked. Nobody else was allowed near the pan."
  > "He made us swear off one lake. Said the fishing was lousy."
  > "It's a simple stove. You pump it eleven times. Not ten. Eleven."

### 3.2 Frank Kosir ("Whiskey"), 72

Retired taconite electrician, Ely. He works nights as a part-time guard at the company's Ely office and says he's
retired. He has a pro-mine sign in the yard. Slovene Ely stock (his *baka*'s potica at Christmas). Gruff, funny,
defensive about his town.
- **Found:** Farm 31 store, **6:10–7:00 a.m.** most weekdays, June–October, buying coffee and a pastry after his shift.
  He wears a grey work shirt with the new sign's patch on the pocket. The Sawbill store, 1–7 August. On the trip.
  At night, on a drive through Ely, under the lights of Northmark's lot (5.7).
- **Wants:** work for his grandkids, and Walt's name kept clean. **Fears:** being thought a traitor.
- **Secret:** in summer 2019, on a night round, he saw a folder on Wade's desk labelled *HAAPALA, W.* He told nobody,
  for the job. It is still in the security office, in the bottom drawer under "Closed".
- **Lies about:** the job. "I'm retired." It's pride, and he's afraid of what the boys would think.
- **Why he looks guilty:** the patch, the electrician who "must" be Sparky, the man in the clean coat he's seen
  talking to in the company's lot at night, and the folder he never mentioned.
- **Gives:** the second voice of 1998. "The Kek. He told me the Kek, summer of '19." (This decodes N34; see 5.13.) When
  cleared and asked, the folder's copies (for Maggie).
- **Voice:** short, blunt, jokes that land like a dropped pack. One Slovene word, once.
  > "I want the mine. I'm not ashamed of it. I want it done by honest men, which is a different thing."
  > "The fishing was not lousy."
  > "I'm retired. I'm extremely retired."

### 3.3 Dennis "Denny" Rask ("Sparky"), 70: the informant

Retired insurance agent from Duluth. He rents a cabin a ten-minute walk back down the Sawbill Trail from the store,
from June to the end of October. A widower since 2014 (new detail; it is why the cabin and the boys are everything).
His son Matt, 39, is alive and well in Duluth, with two girls. Denny is the friendliest man in the game, and the
friendliness is real.
- **Found:** the Sawbill store, **most afternoons** 14:00–17:00, June–October. His cabin, a ten-minute walk down the
  Trail from the store's yard. On the trip. **Only after the player has told him a lake (5.11)**, he turns up once at
  their home outfitter to hear how it went (if home is Sawbill, he's already there), and he calls the outfitter now
  and then to ask after them (5.6).
- **Wants:** forgiveness without confessing. **Fears:** the boys knowing, and Wade.
- **Secret:** all of §2.1 from November 2017, and spring 2026.
- **Lies about:** "Walt's spot was the Isabella." He believes this: it's what Walt fed him. His alibi is true. He was
  in Duluth, and he called from there.
- **Tells (exactly three, §5.12):** the wrong weekday for "that week", given when Bob or Carol asks him in front of the
  player (5.5.1); "he said he was in all week"; "somebody talked".
- **Character, not tells:** he over-helps (rings five places, offers his cabin, leaves his card). He steers to the
  Isabella and to "the mine guys". He asks "You'd bring it to us first, right? We're his guys."
- **The gift (true, and the case's):** he is the one who gets the player onto the August trip, on the boys' own
  permit (5.9.1). And at the second meeting he tells the flare-gun story against himself and decodes **Sparky = Denny**
  before anyone else does (5.6). A guilty man volunteering his own nickname is the most disarming thing in the arc.
- **His card:** *DENNIS RASK · RASK AGENCY · INSURANCE · DULUTH*. The printed office number is crossed out in ballpoint,
  and a home number is written under it: **218-555-0163**. "Call me anytime. I mean anytime. I don't sleep." The
  handwritten number is the one on Marlys's 2019 message pad.
- **Gives:** before the reveal, wrong rings on the Isabella. These pay off with the watchers' 2019 camp (5.10). After
  the reveal, whatever the choice: Wade's name and number, the call, and "they dug the wrong hill on Pietro in '18"
  (lake key 2E). If turned, the decoy on the run.
- **Voice:** warm, quick, too many words on small things and too few on big ones. An insurance man's habit of exact
  numbers. Self-deprecating.
  > "Here, I'll ring it for you. And this one. And there's a carry near there, I'll ring that too."
  > "You think I don't miss him? He was my best friend. He was all our best friend."

### 3.4 Carol Arola, 73

Gene's widow. She works the counter at the Sawbill store (Bev Kangas's; coffee, bars and a pie case beside the leech
cooler) and has rented the apartment over the store since 2017. Forty
carousels of Gene's slides sit in boxes in the store's basement, under the spare life jackets.
- **Found:** the Sawbill store's counter, every day, mid-May to mid-October, 7:00–15:00. After close by
  invitation (5.3).
- **Wants:** to know Gene's paddle is still where Walt left it at Frost. **Fears:** the carousels.
- **Secret:** none. She noticed Denny's money in 2018 and decided it was none of her business.
- **Gives:** the favour (5.2). Gene's September 1998 slide (key 3B). The nicknames as a story (5.4). After the reveal,
  quiet company.
- **Voice:** dry, kind, honest. She gives orders as hospitality.
  > "Gene took a slide of everything. Forty carousels. I can't throw them out and I can't look at them."
  > "Sparky? That was Denny. Flare gun, '86, went off in the canoe. Frank's the electrician, sure. Walt never gave
  > anybody the obvious one."

### 3.5 Offstage

**Gene "Deacon" Arola** (1944–2016) is in slides, in the paddle and in every story. **Matt Rask** (b. 1987) is alive
and well, and never appears. **Frank's daughter** is the 1993 wedding and is never named.
