# Walt's notebook (complete)

Every page of the brown clothbound record book the player finds in the old tent on night one, plus what is tucked
into it: the loose older pages in the back pocket, the photographs, the inside covers and the three torn-out pages
(found later at the camp). Then the night-one sleep cutscene and how the notebook lives in the book afterwards.

Canon: `00-owner-decisions.md`, then `01-bible.md` (section 5.3 above all). Voice: `02a-voice-journal.md`. Places and
ids: `03-places.md`. Where this file had to invent, the detail is listed at the end under **New details (for the
continuity check)**.

**How to read this file**
- Each entry has an id, **NB01–NB88**, in the order the pages fall in the book. Where the bible already drafted the
  entry, its label is given too (**[N06]** etc.). The bible's N-numbers are labels, not order; the NB-numbers are order.
- The line in *italic brackets* above each entry is for designers: the year and weekday (Walt never writes the year),
  the real place, the outfitter reach, the site id, whether it is one of his **starred sites** (★: rated `***` and
  given a white stone on the stump), and what reading it **where he wrote it** gives (bible 6.1).
- The text in the grey block is **finished game text**, in Walt's hand, exactly as the page shows it.
- Inside Walt's text, **square brackets always mean the second ink**: a later line in pencil, added years after,
  drawn in the book's second-ink style (pencil grey, a slightly smaller, shakier hand). Walt never uses brackets
  otherwise. The designer line says when each second-ink line was written.
- Site ids (**C…**) and portage ids (**P…**) are the v3 runtime ids from `03-places.md`. Re-find every one in v9 by
  name and x/z before it goes into code. Rod counts were checked against `public/data/index.json` and
  `src/world-fixes.json`; Walt's own pacing is allowed to differ by a rod or two (P188 is 185 r in the data and 186
  to Walt), never more.
- House style: British spelling in game text (colour, grey, favourite, aluminium).

**Rules every page keeps** (from `02a` and the bible; checked in every entry below)
- One meaning per code word, for the whole notebook. Because of that, Walt's text never uses these words in any other
  sense: *camp* (only the cabin: never "camped", "fish camp"), *cellar*, *far end*, *flowage*, *knob*, *door*,
  *back door*, *ring* (never "fire ring": he says "grate"), *Thursday* (never the weekday), *the river* (only the
  Isabella: other rivers are named), *the other side*, *the line*, *the land man*, *V.* and the initials.
- Every clue line reads as an ordinary trip note to someone who doesn't know the story.
- One clue piece per page at most. No plain directions. No lake name for the far end, ever.
- No names of the boys, the company or the men. No addressing a reader until the last page.

## 1. The object

**The book.** A brown clothbound record book, 192 pages, ruled, the cloth worn pale at the corners and along the
spine where it rode in a lid pocket and later a shirt. About 105 pages are written on: 88 dated entries (some run to a
second page), three torn-out stubs, and the rest blank after the last page. The writing is blue ballpoint for the
quiet and checking years, then mostly pencil from mid-2018 (pens fail in the cold; pencil doesn't), with the
second-ink lines in a softer pencil throughout. The hand is steady and square in 2010 and slowly loosens; by October
2019 the letters lean and the downstrokes shake.

**Inside the front cover**, in ink, top right, small:

```
W.H. 2010–
```

Nothing else. Never closed. The printed "This book belongs to" line underneath is left empty.

**Inside the back cover**, a column of numbers in the bottom left corner, no lake names, no heading. They were added
over the years and the ink shows it: the first three in blue ballpoint, "205/50" in a different blue, the next four in
hard pencil, the last two in soft pencil pressed hard.

```
64
140
186
205/50
50
63
121
214
45
15
```

*[For builders: 64 = P289 Little Trout–Rum; 140 = P645 Snowbank–Disappointment; 186 = P188 Grandpa–Sea Gull (185 r
in the data); 205/50 = P255 + P256, Bald Eagle–Gull; 50, 63, 121, 214 = P530, P27, P24, P26, the run north; 45 =
P47 Carp Portage; 15 = P46 Sucker–Birch. The ink layers date them: quiet years, checking years, 2018, 2019.]*

**The back pocket.** A paper pocket glued inside the back board, the kind record books came with, soft with age. It
holds the loose older pages (section 5) and four of the photographs; the other eight photographs are tucked at the
pages they belong to (section 6).

**The stubs.** Three pages are torn out close to the spine, cleanly, with a ruler's help: the edges are straight. Stub
A follows NB39 (October 2014), stub B follows NB46 (October 2015), stub C sits inside NB63 (October 2017), between its
first day and its second. The pages are in a coffee can under the floorboard at the camp (section 4); when found, each
slots back into its stub.

**Where it was kept** (bible 5.3.1): the pack's lid pocket until June 2018; from then on in Walt's shirt by day and
rolled in the tent's fly at night. NB81 says so. On 17 October 2019 it went into the fly, and stayed there.

### 2.4 Shorthand (as `02a` Part 4)

`r` rods · `P` portage · `L`/`R` landing side · `1x`/`2x` single/double portage · `NW 15`, `g25` wind and gusts in mph ·
`→` wind shifting · `WB` windbound · `T 34` air temp °F at wake · `wt` water temp by hand · `lvl` water level · `*` to
`***` site rating (★ in this file = `***` plus a white stone) · `x` a site passed by · `LT WE NP SMB PS` lake trout,
walleye, northern pike, smallmouth, perch · `rel`/`kept` · `COLO CAJA BAEA WTSP HETH` loon, Canada jay, bald eagle,
white-throated sparrow, hermit thrush · `c. x3` cups of coffee · `hung` food hung · `bf` bear sign · `pty` a party ·
`0 pty` nobody all day · `EP 30` entry point. Finnish, about one word a dozen pages: *perkele* (NB15), *voi voi* (NB30),
*löyly* (NB45), *sisu* (NB48).

## 10. Night one: the sleep cutscene

  word.
- If the player never touches the bundle, the moment they choose to sleep the cutscene begins anyway, with the bundle

Nothing on screen says it matters. No hint card. The cutscene is the sleep.

## 11. The notebook in the book afterwards

### 11.1 Present, read, understood

Nothing in the notebook is locked. It is a physical book: **every page is readable from night one**, including the
second-ink lines, the loose pages and the photographs. What grows is the player's understanding of it, and the book
shows that in three layers.

**1. Present.** All 88 entries, the inside covers, the three stubs, the back pocket (nine loose pages, four photos) and
the eight photos tucked at their pages. Pages turn like the real thing (two-page spreads; NB entries run on where they
run on). A plain contents strip down the side lists each trip's header line as Walt wrote it: date and EP, no year (he
never writes one), in book order.

**2. Read.** A spread counts as read once it has been open about 3 seconds. In keeping with *show only what's missing*,
the book marks the **unread**, not the read: a small pencil dot by each unread header in the contents strip, gone once
read. The game needs a few pages read before some scenes land, and checks for them quietly:
- Tauno's "Walt was a Swede" test (bible 6.3) can be answered only if NB45 (the sauna, *löyly*) has been read.
- The camp search (bible 6.4) reads as a warning only if NB81 has been read; otherwise the player just finds their
  things folded neatly, and the meaning comes when they read NB81 later (the Walt page adds the line then).
- The canary-trap topic is always offered; NB78–NB79 read first make the player likelier to try it, nothing more.

**3. Understood.** When something in the world explains a page, three things happen together, and nothing else:
- **A pencil note in the margin**, in the player's own hand (a rounder pencil than Walt's, distinct from his second ink),
  next to the line it explains: "= Lake One", "Bald Eagle. Where they found Eino.", "Whiskey = Frank". The same word
  elsewhere in the notebook gets a faint underline in that pencil, so a decoded nickname lights up across every page it
  is on.
- **One line on the Walt page** (11.3).
- **Its map effect, if any**: a nickname pencilled on a sheet inks over (bible 5.3.4); a stash, once found, is marked.

**4. Read where he wrote it.** Opening an entry at its place (the book open, E on the page, while standing within the
place's radius) gives the small finding of section 9 or the second-ink line's meaning, and a small mark in Walt's
margin in the player's pencil: *he was here.* Crossing one of the back cover's first-trip carries (64, 140, 186) does the
same for its entry without opening the book (bible 6.3, P2).

**The stubs.** Seen from night one as three torn edges; the book does nothing with them until the player notices (the
Walt page adds "Three pages torn out." the first time a stub's spread is read). When the coffee can is found at the camp,
each page slots into its stub with a strip of old tape, and the Walt page adds one line each.

**Photographs** behave as read/understood too: an unmatched photo has a small pencil dot in its corner; a matched one
has none, and its back gains the player's pencil note of where it is.
