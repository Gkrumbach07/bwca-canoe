story in Farm 31's country. It covers the people, the scenes, the favours that earn information and items, the lies
and how the player can catch them, the set pieces, what the arc feeds into the clue funnel, and how it ends.

## 1. The story in one page

**What it is.** Farm 31 is an old resort landing on Fall Lake at the end of the Ely road. Every morning an
eighty-one-year-old crewman sits on its porch, watching the lot. Three people from one 1970s field office still live
within sight of each other. Tauno Kangas was the crew chief and has the bench on the porch. Mary Pelto was the typist
and has the window table in the café on Tuesdays and Fridays. Len Saari was the manager's field man and has the end
of the dock on most mornings and a lunch table on Thursdays. They nod to each other. In fifty years none of them has
said aloud what each of them knows.

Each of them put one line into the same lie. In September 1976 the creek below the Kettle pad ran orange. The office
decided it had always been that way:
- **Tauno** recopied three days of readings into a clean book so the crew's numbers would agree with the office's.
- **Mary** typed the report from Brekke's pencil.
- **Len** has told Carl Brekke who asked what, every Thursday since. The habit began in 1977 and became paid work in
  2017.

All three did it for something with a face: a house, a husband's good name, a wife's room at the care home. None of
them is a villain. The villain arrives on Thursdays by launch, wears a gold ring, and speaks warmly of everyone.

**The emotional core.** This is Tauno's story. He carried something for forty-nine years. It was not papers. It was
the silence that let the papers stay buried. He kept the clean book. He stood on the landing rock beside the folded
jacket and told the deputy only "He was a careful man." He watched Walt take the ledger and said nothing. In December
1977 he carried Walt's bargain to the land man word for word, and he has never repeated it. When Walt's canoe turned
up upwind in 2019, Tauno read it as a letter to him: *the deal is off.* Then nobody came, for seven years, until a
stranger walked out of a store with BRM-14 on their back.

The arc asks whether an old man gets to set down what he carried, and what it costs him to do it. Its last scene is
Tauno at the slab of the old core shed, where Eino put in at dawn, saying everything once.

**The mirror.** Tasha Strand is thirty-three, a field geologist for the new company and a decent person who wants a
mine done right. In 2025 she was given Tauno's choice: drop it, keep your job, keep your house. The player meets
both of them, the crewman at thirty-one in 1976 and the geologist at thirty-three now. The arc never says so. It lets
the player stand at the orange creek with her one week and at the core-shed slab with him the next.

**The shape, in six movements** (any order where the conditions allow; bible 6.4):
1. **Farm mornings.** Tauno on the porch. Len's free rings. Mary's bars. (Tier 1 begins with Len.)
2. **The Kettle pad after rain.** The set piece. A wet walk up the crews' old grade, then sampling the creek with
   Tasha. The creek still runs orange. Leo's stamped core-box lid is found here.
3. **The back table.** Mary holds Leo's lid and tells what the papers are, what the radio called E-7 and what she has
   been afraid of for fifty years.
4. **The crew map.** Tauno's yield: the region, the crew's target map, the wrong hill. Pietro follows from it.
5. **Thursdays.** The open house and the lunch table: Brekke's one conversation, Dan's sincerity, Len's leak and
   Len's confession on the dock.
6. **The clean book.** The drive to the Little Gabbro road end. Tauno's confession and the choice: ask him to go on
   record when the time comes, or don't.

**What it gives the funnel.** Lock 1 (region) in full, two keys for lock 2 (lake: Mary's far end, the crew map's wrong
hill), keys for locks 5 and 6 (orange-dab blazes, the look of an old pit, core spill, the mirrored sketch), one leak
for the canary trap (Len), the station numbers that let the player needle Brekke, Tauno's call to Maggie, and
Tauno's testimony for the journalist ending. See §6.

## 2. The people of this arc

Ages are for 2026. Only details new to this file are given here; the rest is in bible §3–4.

### 2.1 Tauno Kangas, 81: the porch

- **Schedule:** Farm 31 porch, the bench under the window (never the step), 7–10, June to October. On about one
  morning in five he is at the player's home porch instead, as part of his summer rounds. He drives an old brown
  pickup and does not drive after dark ("My eyes are no good past the junction"). Outside June to October he is at
  Farm only for the opening's scenes (`20-opening.md` 1.1 and 1.3), by daylight.
- **His house.** In 1975 he and his wife **Irene** bought a green house by the ball field in Ely. In September 1976
  Brekke told him that a crew chief who couldn't keep a clean book was a crew chief Minneapolis could replace. He
  still lives in the house. Irene died in 2015. A daughter in the Cities phones him. He mentions the house the way
  other people mention the weather.
- **The clean book's fate.** It went into Boreal's files. Boreal's files went to storage in Minneapolis in 1984, and
  Northmark bought the claim files in 2017. Tauno does not know whether the book survived. He assumes Brekke has it:
  "He keeps things." Nothing in the game settles whether it exists.
- **What he never does:** write on a map, say "Boreal" with the lot full, say Walt is alive, or guess who was on Bald
  Eagle on 12 October 1977. He has thought about every man on the crew camp's party line and names none of them.
- **How he reads the player:** he tests them. He says something small and false and watches whether they correct it.
  He warms one step each time they come back having been somewhere.

### 2.2 Len Saari, 77: the dock and the Thursday table

- **Born 1949, the same year as Eino.** They were hired the same week, late June 1972, the week after Eino turned 23. They were friends for
  a summer. Then Len became Brekke's field man and Eino became the water sampler.
- **Schedule:** the end of Farm's dock most mornings except Thursday, with a rod he barely uses. On Thursdays he is at
  the café's corner table from 11:30. At about 11:45 Brekke's varnished launch comes in from his summer place up Fall
  Lake. Len walks down and takes his line. They eat. At 13:30 the launch goes.
- **Elsie.** His wife Elsie has been in the care home in Ely since 2019. Hap Johnsrud lives in the same home, and the
  two men know each other there. Brekke "helped with the paperwork". The room costs more each month than Len has.
  Len visits Elsie every day except Thursday, and he says so himself. That is a tell (§5).
- **His job.** He is Northmark's "community history" consultant. He collects old photographs and names for the open
  house boards. He is good at it and enjoys it, and that makes everything worse.
- **What he believes about himself:** "I tell him who asked what. That's all." He has said it to himself so often that
  he almost believes it is nothing.

### 2.3 Mary Pelto, 79: the window table

- **Schedule:** Farm 31 café, the window table, Tuesdays and Fridays, 9–11, with a pan of bars she brings for the
  counter girls. In 1976 she was the office typist, and she also ran the field radio schedule with the fly camps,
  mornings and evenings.
- **Leo.** Her husband was Leo Pelto, the best driller on the belt by his own account, who died in 1981. He stamped
  his initials and the date on the end of every core box he filled, with a punch set that had been his father's:
  *L.P.* and the month and year.
- **Her real fear.** Bible §3 gives the bribed state water inspector the initials **"L.P."**. Mary once saw
  Sorensen's green ledger open on her own desk: two letters and a number. Leo was fighting with Brekke that winter
  about the pads. She has been afraid for fifty years that if the ledger ever surfaces, the town will read "L.P." as
  Leo. Leo never took a dime. This fear is why she wants Leo's name kept clean and why she fears court (bible §4.2).
- **The office typewriter.** The big grey office machine had a tired **e** that sat a hair low on the line.
  Everything typed in that office carries it: the 1976 water reports in the state's files, and also the carbon in
  Sorensen's folder. In late August 1977 Brekke did his own typing, which he never did. Mary found her ribbon moved on
  in the morning.

### 2.4 Tasha Strand, 33: the creek

- **Schedule:** with a Northmark sampling truck on the forest road below the Kettle pad, and at the Little Gabbro
  road end (EP 33), on weekdays from June to October. Some mornings she is at Farm 31's store, buying ice for the
  coolers.
- **Who she is (bible §4.2):** Ojibwe on her grandmother's side (no band named), from Tower. Michigan Tech. Northmark's field geologist since 2022. She is
  guarded with strangers and easy with anyone who has carried a cooler for her.
- **Her own book.** She keeps a personal field book of everything she samples, at home, apart from the company's
  database. Nobody told her to do this. In 2025 she wrote an internal note saying the 1976 "background" stations were
  dirtier than the upstream ones. She was told it was a closed question. She still has the note.
- **She never** gives away anything that isn't public, and never tells a stranger the internal story. She tells the
  player only on a second meeting, after they have worked a day for her (§4.4).

### 2.5 Carl Brekke, 88: the ring

- **Seen:** at the Thursday table with Len, June to October, and arriving by launch. At Northmark's open house at Farm
  on the fourth Saturday of August, shaking hands by the coffee urn. Never on foot anywhere else, and never alone with
  the player.
- **Manner:** warm and slow. He speaks of Walt as the best line-cutter he ever had and of Eino as "a lovely boy, a
  hothead". He never asks a direct question. He shows the player that he knows things by mentioning them kindly. He
  turns the gold mining-school ring with his thumb when he is pleased. When he is not pleased, the thumb stops.
- **What he knows about Tauno:** everything, and he has never needed to use it. When the player has been seen with
  Tauno, he says the arc's coldest line, and it is a compliment: "Give my best to Tauno. He kept a lovely book."

### 2.6 Dan Marolt, 41: the open house

As in bible §4.6. In this arc he runs the open house. He is proud of the boards and he buys real junk, including,
if offered, Leo's lid (§4.3).

### 2.7 Offstage, as this arc uses them

- **Eino.** A joker in reports ("He once logged a moose as a station"). He pulled the draft out of the burn barrel in
  1976 while Mary watched and went back to her typing. On the last Monday of August 1977 he was "spoken to" behind
  Brekke's shut door for an hour. Len drove him back.
- **Leo Pelto.** He drilled where he was told and logged what he was told, and said so as a joke. His boxes are
  still out at the pads, rotting, his initials on the ends.
- **Dale Sorensen** (d. 2009). Tauno went to his funeral. "I don't know why." (Aili's version of that funeral is in
  33-family.)
- **Irene Kangas** (d. 2015), **Elsie Saari** (in the care home). Mentioned, never seen.
