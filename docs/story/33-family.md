# The family arc: Aili's coffee

The fourth of the Act II groups (bible 6.4): Walt's family at **Little Vermilion 12**, the Crane Lake landing on the
far west border. It yields **the landing sign** (lock 4) and hides **that Walt lived** (5.6). This file is the full
regional story: the country, the people, how their trust is earned and lost, every scene with its branches, the
notebook pages that belong to this country, what the family does in each ending, and what has to be built.

Canon order: `00-owner-decisions.md`, then `01-bible.md` (sections 2.5–2.6, 4.5, 5.2 lock 4, 5.4, 5.6, 6.4 "The
family", 7). Voices: `02a-voice-journal.md` for Walt's pages, `02b-voice-people.md` (3.3 "the frightened", 6.9) for
everyone here. Places: `03-places.md` section 4; ids below are v3 and must be looked up again in v9 by name and x/z.
Lines in this file are finished game text unless marked as a stage direction `[in brackets]` or a designer note.

## 1. The arc in one page

**Who.** **Aili Niemi**, 74, Walt's sister, who runs the motel, bait and coffee counter beside the Little Vermilion
outfitter. **Jon Niemi**, 45, her son, a fishing guide on Crane and Sand Point. **Liisa Niemi**, 22, Jon's daughter,
season staff at the outfitter. Around them: **Gord Pelletier**, the Atikokan outfitter who brings his boat across Sand
Point on Thursdays, and the Little Vermilion keeper.

**What they know.** Aili knows Walt is alive. She read the upwind canoe in October 2019, the way he meant her to, and
a Christmas card has come every December since 2020 with no words in it and a pencil drawing of a stone on a stump.
Jon suspects and has never asked her: a Quetico ranger told him over a beer about an abandoned canoe, and he has never
repeated it. Liisa knows nothing. She thinks the cards are from a friend of Grandma's.

**What they fear.** That the men who came to the bunkhouse will find Walt through them. Aili is also afraid it will
take Jon and Liisa with him. In 1977 she sat in her mother's kitchen while a man from the office talked about the
weather for an hour, and a month later her younger brother was dead in Bald Eagle. She has spent forty-nine years not
saying things, and seven of them keeping a second brother alive by never saying so.

**What the player gets** (all of it earned, none of it asked for twice):
- **The landing sign**, lock 4 key A: "He always put a white stone on the stump where he landed. Our dad did that."
- **Liisa's cards**: lock 4 key D (the drawing), and one of the five "went north" pieces (5.6 no. 2: Ontario
  postmarks from December 2020).
- **Eino's last morning**: the ducks with no shotgun, "a man at the paper in Duluth", and the kitchen hour of 1977.
  These are facts for the case page and for Maggie.
- **The memorial guest book**, with *W. Helstrom* in it: the company's fingerprint on 2019.
- **Jon's protection**: who else was on Sand Point that day (a rented boat, and a man in clean boots on the Crane
  dock), and a warning whenever a truck sits at the landing.
- From Gord, met through this country: the American who paid cash for a winter camp (5.6 no. 5).

**What they never give.** Walt's route, his town, his name in Canada, a confirmation. Nobody in this family ever says
Walt is alive, to anyone, in any ending. The postmarks are the closest the player gets to an address, and six towns
across north-western Ontario are a region, not a place. The final postcard comes to the player directly (7.3), never
through Aili.

**Shape.** Arrive (Aili's coffee, Jon's back) → stay (her other stories, the board, the photo on the wall) → a gift
she never asked for (Mummo's lilacs or June's pot) → the stone → the dead brother, whom she can talk about → the guest
book → the Sand Point chase and Jon → Liisa and the cards → **the coffee can on the porch**, with its choice.

**Climax.** The player is on Aili's back porch with the coffee can of cards in their hands, and Aili is home early.
**The choice:** tell her "I know", or hand the can back and say Liisa thought she'd want them in the house. In the
other arcs a person confesses at the climax. Here the can of cards does the confessing, and Aili still says nothing.

## 2. The emotional core

Aili will talk about the brother who is dead. She will not talk about the one who isn't.

That is the whole arc. Eino has been dead forty-nine years. Nothing she says can hurt him, so once she trusts the
player she will tell everything she has: the gun on the hooks, the man in the kitchen, the man at the paper. Walt is
alive. One word about him could reach the wrong men, so she says nothing, ever, and she is good at it. She keeps the
porch light on every night. She keeps the cards in a coffee can behind the boots. She refused his pack and tent at the
memorial so that she could truthfully say she had nothing of his. When the player walks into her bait shop carrying
that same tent, the thing she sent away has come back to her door on a stranger's back.

So the test of this arc is not getting Aili to talk. It is the player learning to stop asking. Trust here is earned
by doing something kind that asks nothing back, and by showing that you can know a thing without saying it. That is
also what Walt asked of her, and of Ollie and Tauno, when he put his canoe on the upwind shore: read this, and say
nothing. The coffee-can choice is where the player either joins the family's silence or breaks it.

Jon is the same love with the anger left in. He was Walt's nephew, and Walt taught him to fish the wind side and
never to tell anyone where he landed. He follows strangers on the lake because somebody has to. Liisa is the
generation that was protected so well that she doesn't know what she is protecting. She wants her great-uncle, and the
only person who can give him to her is a stranger with his notebook. Each of the three costs the player something
different in the endings: Aili's trust, Jon's door, Liisa's cards.

**Motifs** the builders and writers keep in step:
- **Aili never sits.** She pours the player's cup and stays standing behind the counter, wiping something. She sits
  down across from the player exactly once, at the end of the journalist ending if they kept Walt out of print and
  had told her "I know" (7.1: "she pours the second cup without a word").
- **The second cup.** Walt's N02 pours a second cup into the lake out of habit. Aili always pours one cup. The
  postcard in the best ending says "Two cups this morning. Drank both."
- **The porch light.** On every night since October 2019 (the motel's own porch, by the back door). Walt's Sand Point
  page notes "A.'s porch light on when I passed." She never explains it: "It's a motel. People come in late."
- **"Anything of his."** Her one precise wording: "I don't have anything of his." It is true. The cards are not his.
  They are hers, from him.

## 3. The country

**Little Vermilion 12.** Remote country, Quetico's door: big border water, motorboats outside the wilderness line,
long days to anywhere. Crane Lake is the end of the road. It is a place whose people moved there when the resorts
inside were bought out: motorboats, walleye, the Park next door, the least sentimental town on the map about canoes.
The family arc is the reason most players first come this far west, and its favours send them all the way back east
to Basswood and Alice. The arc is meant to cross the map.

## 4. The people

The bible's entries (4.5, 4.10) stand. What follows adds what a writer needs to play each one in scenes.

### Aili Niemi, 74 — *the sister*

- Born 1952 in Ely, the youngest Haapala. Married **Pekka Niemi** in 1980 and moved to Crane Lake, where his people
  had come down with what they had after the buyouts. The two of them ran the motel and the bait tank. Pekka died in
  2012. She runs it alone and does not want help.
- **The kitchen.** In September 1977, at 25, she sat in her mother's kitchen while Dale Sorensen talked about the
  weather for an hour. She has not trusted weather talk from a stranger since, which is awkward for a woman who sells
  bait. Helmi never made anybody coffee in that cup again.
- **The morning.** On 12 October 1977 she went out to the porch for the milk and saw Eino's shotgun on the hooks.
  The night before, he had told her he was going to see a man at the paper in Duluth on Friday, and not to tell Ma.
  She told Walt about the gun. Walt said leave it. She left it.
- **2019.** She read the upwind canoe the day Sheila's office called: a south wind, a south shore. She said "Thank
  you for calling" and hung up and sat on the porch step until it was dark. At the memorial she refused the box: "I
  don't want it in the house. If they ask, I have nothing." Men nobody knew signed the guest book. She kept it.
- **Since.** The porch light. The cards, in a coffee can behind the boots on the porch shelf. A car that went past at
  seven and at five every day of that first winter, and late mail. She tells no one anything, including her son.
- **How she withholds** (`02b` 3.3, the frightened). Narrow answers to wide questions. Hospitality as a wall: coffee,
  bars, a stool, a job ("I have to get the wash in"). One slip into the present tense about Walt, corrected once in
  the whole arc. She never lies outright. She omits, and her omissions are worded exactly.
- **What she'll talk about freely:** the bait, the lake, the motel guests, Pekka, the bootleggers (Pekka's father's
  stories, S6's hook), Mummo's camp before the buyout, Eino once she trusts you. Never Walt, never Canada, never where
  the player is going.
- **Voice:** "Coffee? There's bars. Sit. I'm not going to talk about him, but sit." / "Don't tell me where you're
  going. I don't want to know where." / "A man sat in my mother's kitchen for an hour once and talked about the
  weather. So you'll forgive me."

### Jon Niemi, 45 — *the son*

- Born 1981. Grew up in the bait shop. Walt was the uncle who came at Christmas with smoked fish, taught him to read a
  wind line on Sand Point, and slept in Pekka's chair after dinner. Jon has guided Crane and Sand Point since he was
  twenty and goes over the line often with his papers in order.
- He doesn't know what Walt and Eino had. He knows his mother leaves the porch light on, and he knows what a ranger
  told him about an abandoned aluminium canoe in October 2019. He has never said that to anyone and never will. He
  has never asked his mother, either: "We don't talk about it. That's how we talk about it."
- He has seen a rented boat with two men and no rods on Sand Point since the player started coming around. That is
  why he follows the player: to see whether they are with it.
- **Lies about:** following the player (6.4's false watcher). His tell is that he knows where they went.
- **Voice:** "My mother's had enough people at the door. You're one more." / "Wasn't me you needed to worry about out
  there."

### Liisa Niemi, 22 — *the granddaughter*

- Born 2004. She was fifteen in 2019; she remembers a great-uncle at Christmas who smelled of woodsmoke and let her
  win at cribbage badly enough that she knew. Season staff at the Little Vermilion outfitter: counter, dock, the
  shuttle van.
- She knows Grandma keeps Christmas cards in a coffee can and keeps the envelopes, which Grandma never does with
  anything. She thinks they come from "a friend of Grandma's from before". She does not know the white stone means
  anything.
- She wants stories. She is the only one in the family who asks about Walt out loud, and she has learned not to ask
  at home.
- **Lies about:** nothing. She is careless, kind and a little bored.
- **Voice:** "Grandma says he drowned. Grandma also keeps Christmas cards in a coffee can, so." / "There's never any
  writing. Just the little drawing. A stone on a stump. Every year."

### Gord Pelletier, 66 — *the Thursday boat*

- Atikokan outfitter and fly-in camp man (bible 4.10). He comes across Sand Point on Thursdays for the mail and the
  gas and twenty minutes of talk at the outfitter's dock. He knows Aili to nod to and buys nothing at her counter. He
  has no idea who the American was and does not care to.
- **Voice:** "This was a mine town too. They closed around '80. We know how that goes." / "He said thank you twice.
  Americans say it once. Finns say it twice."

### The Little Vermilion keeper

The existing generated keeper of EP 12 (`outfitters.js`, seeded by the entry point). Not renamed here. In this arc
they ring places, carry two notes, and say the one line about Aili everyone in Crane Lake says: "She's all right. She's
had a lot of people at the door."

### Offstage

Pekka Niemi (d. 2012: a photo behind the counter, in the boat, laughing at something outside the frame). Helmi and
Toivo. Mummo. June. Eino. The ranger who told Jon, never named.

## 5. Trust

There is no meter. The player sees only what changes: whether she pours the coffee before they ask, whether she says
"Sit", whether the cup is the good one or the motel one, what she talks about, and how long before she says she has to
get the wash in. Builders keep a hidden standing per person (the keepers' `rep` pattern in `npcs.js`), but it is never
shown.

### Aili's door

| Step | How it's reached | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| **0 · At the counter** | First visit | Sells bait. Pours coffee in a motel mug. Narrow answers about Walt. |
| **1 · Sit** | Two days of visits, **or** one visit carrying his tent | "Sit." The good mug. Talks about anything but him: the lake, Pekka, the photo on the wall, the bootleggers. Opens the photo-on-the-wall topic, which plants S7 and S9. |
| **2 · The stone** | Step 1, then **a gift she never asked for**: the June photo of Mummo's lilacs (S7), **or** June's coffee pot shown to her (S9). (Or, if the cards came first, the kind answer at the porch: section 6.9) | "I'll tell you one thing and then you'll go." The white stone. |
| **3 · The kitchen table** | Step 2, the player knows Eino's name, a day's gap | Eino's last morning; the kitchen hour; then the guest book. |
| **After the can** | The climax | "Thank you" path: coffee for good, nothing said. "I know" path: cold for the rest of the season. |

**What helps.** Visit days (each day you talk to her counts once). Buying bait (once a day counts as a visit).
Finishing one of her two board notices (6.13). Carrying the tent in. Accepting the coffee and the bars (declining
them three times in a row reads as unfriendly and costs a visit's worth; she says nothing about it).

**What sets her back** (each one closes her for the rest of that day: "I have to get the wash in."):
- Asking "Is he alive?" a **second** time in a season. The first time she answers narrowly. The second: "That's
  twice." The third time in any season drops her a step for three game days.
- Telling her where the player is going next. "Don't tell me where you're going. I don't want to know where." (She
  is not part of the canary trap.)
- Offering to show her the notebook. "Don't. If I don't see it, I don't know it."
- Repeating Gord's story about the American to her. "Gord talks. Thursdays he talks. I don't."
- Bringing up Canada in any form: "There's nothing up there but Canadians."
- Saying Walt's name in the store when someone else is at the counter. She pours their coffee and doesn't look at
  the player again until the other person leaves.

**The teaching moment** (once, the first time she redirects). The Walt page writes, in the player's words: *Aili, his
sister. Crane Lake, the motel at the Little Vermilion landing. She'll talk about anything but him.* That is the only
time the game tells the player how she works. After that they learn it from her.

### Jon

Closed until **one** of: the player shows him the last page (6.7), or tells him after the informant reveal that one of
Walt's friends sold him (6.7). Before that, every conversation is short and hostile, and he denies the chase. After it
he becomes protection (6.8). Nothing the player does with Aili moves Jon, and the other way round, except that "I know"
on the porch makes Jon cold too, for the same season.

### Liisa

Opens by stories. Each **Walt story** the player tells her (6.10) moves her one step. She shows the can after **two**
stories if one of them was read to her from the notebook in his hand, or **three** if the player never says where they
know the stories from. She only shows it when Aili is out (Mondays, 9–16).

   Crown land, not the park; Quetico's west door is Lac La Croix, up the Loon River and over the Beatty. This file
