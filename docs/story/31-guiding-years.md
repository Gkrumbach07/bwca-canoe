# 31 · The guiding years: Moose Lake, the counters, and page two

The guiding-and-outfitting arc of Act II (bible 6.4, "The guiding years"). These are the people who worked beside Walt
across fifty years of other people's trips: an old guide, a counter woman, a deputy who searched for him, the boy who
swamped for him, and the stores where he left his pencil on the wall. It covers the people, the scenes, the
favours and tasks, the lies and their tells, the two set pieces, the one photo favour, what the arc gives the clue
funnel, and how it ends.

Canon: `00-owner-decisions.md`, then `01-bible.md`. Voices: `02a-voice-journal.md` (Walt), `02b-voice-people.md`
(everyone else). Places: `03-places.md`. Sibling files read for agreement: `20-opening.md` (when Marlys may first
appear; the Moose Lake keeper bought the store in 2021) and `40-company-and-press.md` (Wade's scenes, the fallback

## 1. The arc in one paragraph

**Moose Lake 25. Gentle, busy. Yields the lake, and holds the hinge.** At the busiest landing in the wilderness, the
people who knew Walt as a working man are still about: **Hap Johnsrud**, who guided beside him, sits on the porch at
weekends and can't go out any more; **Marlys Lind**, who ran the bunkhouse he vanished from, volunteers at the counter
on Tuesdays and Wednesdays and says she doesn't remember much; **Sheila Novak**, the deputy who wrote up his search,
keeps the rescue squad's boat at the landing. Hap gives the player a day from 1986 to paddle and asks what's there now;
one of his places is wrong, and setting him right the way a friend would earns the thing he never told the sheriff:
where Walt went every October (lock 2, the lake). Sheila gives the player page two of her report, the wind, and on a
south-wind night lets them prove on Birch Lake that Walt's canoe could not have drifted where it was found (the hinge:
"drowned" becomes "went north"). Marlys sends the player up a ladder into the boathouse loft for the 2019 register, and
with it in their hands tells the night she has carried for seven years. Then the player decides whether her story is
written down by someone who will keep it, or left to lie where the sheriff left it.

Around Moose Lake, smaller threads run back through Walt's other counters: a 1981 guide photo on Farm's wall, his
pencilled rods on Hog Creek's old wall map, his and his brother's initials cut into a Moose Lake rafter in 1961 and 1964.

## 2. The emotional core: page two

Everybody in this arc is good at noticing. It's the trade. A guide reads the wind before the map; a counter woman knows
every regular by his coffee; a rescue deputy writes the weather down because the weather is where a search starts. And
every one of them, once, stopped at page one:

- **Hap**, in the fall of 1977, told Walt to let it go. Walt did, for forty years, and came for coffee once a month
  to the end, and never said whether that meant Hap was right or forgiven.
- **Marlys** boxed Walt's things at six in the morning on instinct, lied to the man in clean boots, and then did what
  the sheriff told her: don't make it worse. She has looked at a parking lot every night since.
- **Sheila** wrote the wind on page two and filed it, and nobody read page two, and she didn't make them.

Walt was a guide. A guide's whole job, Rick says, is to *hook it and hand them the rod, and never say*. On his last
night Walt guided one more time: he wrote the wind at the top of his last page, put his canoe on the shore the wind
couldn't put it on, and left the reading to the people who would look. Three of them were in this arc's country and
didn't. The player does.

So the arc gives each of them their page two back. Hap gets his places seen again and his one error set right, the way
Walt never would. Sheila gets her wind read, out loud, on the water. Marlys gets to tell her night, in bunk
3, hour by hour, while the player lives it (the phone at five past six, a truck idling in the lot, the stuck door), and
the player chooses for her what she chose for herself in 2019: **bring it in, or let it lie.** That phrase belongs to this arc.
Hap says it about 1977, Marlys says it about 2019, and it is the arc's last choice.

What the player should feel at the end: that these were decent, frightened, competent people, that the man they failed
to look after was the one who looked after everybody, and that he found a way to be looked after anyway.

## 4. The country and the calendar

**Home ground:** Moose Lake 25 (EP 25, quota 27, "half of Iowa in July"). Its reach: Snowbank, Lake One, Wood, North
Kawishiwi (`campaigns.md`). The arc's water is the Moose–Newfound–Sucker chain (all one level), Prairie Portage (P194,
122 r) into Basswood's Inlet Bay, and Birch Lake on the border by the Sucker–Birch bypass (P46, 15 r). Everything in
the arc is a **free day trip** from the Moose Lake dock except the drift test (a night "with the squad", no permit) and
anything the player chooses to make longer.

**Who is where, when** (weekday from `absDay % 7`, as the parties already do):

Nobody in this arc dies, moves away or closes a door. Hap is back every June. A player who misses Hap's summer finishes
his day next summer, or takes the lake key from Mary, the rods, the wrong hill, the chart or Denny (bible 5.2).

**Season fit.** Hap's day is a summer piece (June for the lilacs at Mummo's, any weekend for the rest). The drift test
wants a south wind at night, which is mostly a late-summer and fall thing; the arc's climax can fall anywhere.

## 5. People

### 5.1 Marlys Lind, 71 · *the bunkhouse*

Counter manager at Moose Lake 1998–2021; ran the bunkhouse; lived in the house behind the store, whose kitchen window
looks at the lot. Since the sale she lives in town and volunteers Tuesdays and Wednesdays "because Gus can't price a
leech". Widow; no children; a red canoe she hasn't paddled in years stored in the boathouse rafters.

- **Wants:** to know she did right by Walt. **Fears:** the man who asked for his things. **Secret** (bible 4.3): she
  carried the 6:05 message to bunk 3; saw the truck at one; heard the door at two; boxed the gear at six; lied to Wade
  that Walt's sister took it all; kept the message.
- **Lies about:** "I don't remember much about that week." *Why:* the sheriff said no foul play and not to make it
  worse for the family; and Wade. *Tells:* she remembers everything else to the day and the dime (the burn hole in the
  fly, the year, the quarters); she corrects dates nobody asked her to; and she looks at the lot. **Her one recurring
  stage action** is a look out the front window at the parking lot, once a conversation. It is the arc's only physical
  tell and it stops only at the end (section 12).
- **Gives:** how the gear reached the player; the Front Room; "M." in N39; the register and the message (GY-12); the
  night (GY-13); the fallback call to Maggie (`40-company-and-press.md` 6.2).
- **Voice:** practical, quick, store-proud, kind in actions rather than words. She says "Walt", never "Mr. Haapala",
  and never "the brother".
  - "When the place sold, the old stuff went in the lot. Half the back rooms on the Fernberg have something of ours."
  - "He paid for his bunk in quarters and fixed the dock for nothing. I'd have kept his gear forever."

### 5.2 Hap Johnsrud, 84 · *the old guide*

Guided Basswood and Moose 1958–2002; partnered Walt in the late 1960s and the 1980s. In the care home in Ely since 2010.
Norwegian stock from Winton, "a Lutheran who swears". A cane he uses to point.

- **Wants:** one more look at his places. **Fears:** being forgotten. **Secret** (bible 4.3): in the fall of 1977 he
  told Walt to let it go. Walt came for coffee every month from 2010 to 2019 anyway.
- **Lies about:** nothing. His memory has holes and he says so. **The hole that matters:** he puts the Prairie tow
  landing on the wrong side (GY-05b). It's honest. Walt knew and never corrected him (GY-N2).
- **Gives:** the 1986 day (set piece A); the snapshot (the arc's one photo favour); the lake (bible 5.2, lock 2 key A);
  the Front Porch, the Kitchen and the Long Room; "never more than a rod off" (Walt's pacings); the lilacs as a lead to Aili.
- **Voice:** slow, dry, fond, exact about water and vague about years.
  - "Walt? He guided out of three places I know of. Never stayed. Good with the clients, didn't like the owners."
  - "He'd go out alone in October. People said he was strange. I said he liked October."

### 5.3 Sheila Novak, 61 · *page two*

Lake County deputy 1990–2022, rescue squad volunteer since 1991; Ollie Pekkala taught her to run a drag line. She
overlapped with Wade in the department.

- **Wants:** someone to read page two. **Fears:** being the one who missed it. **Secret** (bible 4.3): she kept a copy
  of her report and the weather record in the squad boat's first-aid box, and she knows who told the office "old man,
  high wind".
- **Lies about:** nothing. She withholds names in public: "I'm not going to say a name on a dock."
- **Gives:** page two (GY-09); the overdue party (GY-10, optional, teaches reading the calm side); the ride and the

- **Voice:** dry, plain, a little gallows; counts things.

  - "We overlapped two years, Wade and me. He kept his boots clean then too."

### 5.4 Nate Johnsrud, 27 · *the grandson*

Hap's grandson. Drives Moose Lake's tow boat weekdays and his grandfather weekends. Teases Hap and protects him in the

### 5.5 Rick Pasanen, 54 · *the swamper*

From Babbitt. Swamped for Walt at Moose Lake 1986–89, from fourteen; took Hap's 1986 snapshot (and cut off Hap's feet).
