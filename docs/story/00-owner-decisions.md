# The main story: the owner's decisions (canon)

Settled with the owner across 2026-11-02..05. This file is the source of truth: every story document must agree with
it. Where a later document invents detail, it must not contradict anything here.

## Tone and scope

- A calm canoe game with a real mystery under it. It is never forced: you can paddle every lake and ignore the story.
  It is always there.
- History shapes the land, the outfitters and the people. **We do not retell exact real events or name real
  people as characters.** Real history is texture: ruins, grudges, yard signs, the way old-timers talk. All names,
  the company and the crimes are fictional. Background research: `docs/plans/bwca-history-research.md`.
- The villains are **specific men who did corrupt things through a mining company**, not mining itself. Decent people
  stand on both sides of the mining question.
- No Ojibwe sacred material, no Wendigo, no spirit beings. Real tragedies only by their shape, never real victims.
- The player is **just a person going up to the Boundary Waters.** No owner, no geologist, no backstory. They keep
  coming back because they want to figure it out: it is a mystery, and then they are in too deep.

## How the player gets the notebook

- Busy season at the player's chosen outfitter: rentals are out. A regular staffer (not the owner) digs out old gear
  from the back that never gets rented because it's old. It works, just not modern. "If you're not going to need it."
  The player takes it: free gear.
- The pack and tent were **Walt's**. They sat on a shelf in the back for years, pushed aside, unused. Ownership
  shuffled, staff came and went, the tag is long gone. Nobody knows what's in them.
- **The first night** of the first trip (whatever route the player picks; not Walt's route), the player finds the
  notebook in the tent. **The sleep cutscene introduces it**: reading by headlamp, an old man's hand, years of calm
  solo trips, then the last page.

## What happened to Walt (the truth)

- **Walt and his brother** worked for a mining company's exploration crews (1970s). The upper owners knew little of
  the boots-on-the-ground work; the miners knew the spots. **The brothers swiped documents** proving the company's
  corruption and stashed them in a cache deep in the woods, in the area where the crews used to drill/mine. Only the
  two of them knew the exact location.
- **The brother** was the one who wanted to expose them. Walt warned him; threats happened; the brother died. It was
  framed as going out alone and drowning. **Who killed the brother is never known**: it is assumed the company sent
  some people out or hired some gang. The cache was never found.
- Walt spent decades living his life, trying to escape it (guiding, outfitting, different outfitters, trips with
  "the boys", then many solo trips).
- **The company came back** (same corruption, new name, some of the same men). Only Walt knew; morally he had to act.
  His later solo trips were: checking the cache was still there (it was), and **building an escape plan** for the day
  it gets exposed: putting things in places, building up. It started to look suspicious. People started watching him.
- **One random night**, staying in the outfitter's bunkhouse, he knew someone was coming for him. He wrote in the
  notebook (the last entry looks **rushed, incomplete, scrambling**: he had to go immediately), stuffed it in his tent,
  left his gear, and went out in the night in his canoe.
- People only know: he was in his bunkhouse one day and gone the next. His gear was still there; his canoe was gone.
  His canoe was found **swamped, no paddle**. He was **presumed drowned**. No foul play was noted, so the search never
  went far and his gear was never investigated. It sat on a shelf for **5–10 years** (he vanished roughly 2016–2021;
  the bible picks the year).
- In reality he **overturned his canoe to fake his death**, fled to Canada, changed his name and disappeared.
  **He never got the cache**: they were after him and he couldn't risk revealing its location. It is still there.
- **We never meet Walt.** At the end there is only a postcard.

## The hook and the campaign start

- From the last page the player knows little except: there is something hidden in the woods that some group of
  people want, and Walt was scared.
- Back at the outfitter, the front desk knows nothing. **Walking out, an old man stops you** (just hanging out). He
  knew the brothers and worked for the mining company. He gives hints and a little history but is super secretive and
  paranoid: the company was corrupt, the brothers had evidence, the brother didn't die randomly (murder), and he
  doesn't believe Walt died either. **This starts the campaign.** The player chooses to investigate.

## The investigation

- Talking to people, in groups: **old-timers who knew Walt at different times of his life**: those from the mining
  company days, those from his outfitting days, **"the boys"** (his trip friends), and **his family**.
- **His family** (some of them) know he got out and is in Canada. They would never let that leak: there is a lot of
  fear.
- **The old company is still present** in the area. You never know who was who. Who's lying, who's not, who's lying
  for what reason.
- **One of the boys is the informant** (the owner: "adds a nice twist").
- Walt never wrote plain directions, to keep things secret even if the journal was found. The player pieces the
  journal together with what people say.
- There are big events and small events, big findings and small findings.
- Leads work like The Witcher: once you discover something, you get next steps (a person, a place; a pencil ring on
  the map). Not a step list.
- **Sub-campaigns in the middle** help you get information or items. Walt went out alone a lot, so his notebook has
  things he saw (e.g. a Sasquatch encounter); following his trail, you are bound to see these things too. Cool little
  mini games and encounters.

## The ending

- **The secret portage:** an unmapped portage that leads not to another lake but to a rundown, abandoned shack/cabin
  Walt built himself over years of solo trips, **old mining equipment holding it up**. Overgrown, gone unnoticed (or
  ransacked: the bible decides). Clues there; you know you're close. Then **you find the cache.** It is very hard to
  work out which region and lake this portage is on, but eventually you do.
- **A journalist/reporter**, met while building up to this, is your way to whistleblow. You set a meeting point at
  another outfitter or entry point.
- **The run:** on your way out, strange canoeists see you and radio out. A race to the meeting point: sneak, hide,
  pull off, paddle through day and night, **no campsites**, flashlight on and off. A spooky stealth sequence.
- **No "you got caught, you lose" ending.** If caught, the player resets to the last night (or similar).
- **No "put it back" ending** (anticlimactic). By then you are too deep in it: you have to choose.
- **Endings:** give the cache to the journalist → exposed, too late for foul play, a happy ending; or give it to the
  company for a bribe (lots of money). **Only these two endings** (2026-11-05: the owner cut the bible's third,
  "both hands", photos to the journalist and papers to the company). Variations inside an ending (e.g. whether Walt
  is named to the journalist) are fine; never "put it back" or "lose".
- After the ending, the game continues; the world shows what you did. A postcard from Walt (unsigned, Canada).

## Writing

- Language guide from real bwca.com trip reports and forum posts, for both the journal and the people.
- Full campaign with fully thought-out side quests. Cross-checked so things line up and are engaging.


## 2026-11-08: owner answers to the campaign design's calls

- **Endings' end games.** *Journalist:* the camp always stays; the outfitter owners, very happy with you, give you secret
  missions afterwards (a giant fish in one spot on some lake, a cave with something good in it, other secrets).
  *Company money:* lots of money, but every outfitter upcharges you; it softens owner by owner when you do one end-game
  task for each (a donation, a job), about a year to win them all back. Still only these two endings.
- **Co-op:** a guest is only there to help; their own save is separate and does not move. There is one notebook, the
  host's; a guest can read it but never holds it. Tasks are shared. The cellar night is the host's alone (no company).
- **A catch** restores food and energy to the checkpoint (the last night), keeping anything found.
- **The late season:** permits self-issued and free from 1 October; ice can catch you (walk/carry out, or a costly
  evacuation); "Look at next year's permits" on the desk (forward in fall): choosing one ends the open-water season and skips to that date next year.

  overnight. The two days after keep the season's own weather, at most light rain.
