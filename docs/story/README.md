# The story: Walt's notebook

The main campaign and side stories for the BWCA canoe game. Everything here is fiction set on the real Boundary Waters
and Quetico map. People, the companies and the crimes are invented; real history is background only.

## The campaign on one page

**The gear.** It's busy season and the rentals are all out. At the counter of the outfitter the player picks, Josie,
the summer help, digs out an old canvas pack stencilled BRM-14 and an A-frame tent from the back room. Nobody knows
whose they were. On the first night of the first trip, a brown notebook slides out of the tent's rolled fly. It holds
ten years of an old man's solo trips, written in his own nicknames. The last page was written in a hurry: *Out the door
before they … Not the cellar. Don't go near the … If you are reading this*.

**The porch (Act I).** Walking out of an outfitter, the player is stopped by Tauno Kangas, 81, who once crewed for
Boreal Range Minerals in the 1970s. He knows the patch: it was Walt Haapala's crew pack. Walt "drowned" in 2019. Tauno
says the company was crooked, and that Walt's brother went into the water too. He sends the player to the crews' old
landing on Bald Eagle. When the player comes back, he names the brother, Eino, who drowned in October 1977. Eino's
canoe was found swamped, with no paddle, on the upwind shore. Then Tauno opens four doors.

**The four groups (Act II, any order).** Each group is a regional arc with a set piece and a choice:
- **The mining crew at Farm.** They give the region, the Gabbro–Bald Eagle belt. One old crewman, Len, still reports
  to the company every Thursday.
- **The guiding years at Moose Lake.** They give the lake, and the night Walt left. Hap remembers Walt going "to the
  far end" every October. Marlys kept the bunkhouse register and the phone message. Deputy Sheila's ignored page two
  gives the wind. In the drift test the player lets a canoe go on Birch Lake, and it fetches up on the Canadian shore,
  never where Walt's was found. Walt went north.
- **The boys at Sawbill.** They give the shore: the 1998 trip to Gull's east bay and the beaver dam. They also hide the
  informant. Denny ("Sparky") took the company's money to pay for his son's cancer treatment, and on the last night he
  made the phone call. Frank, the obvious suspect, is a red herring.
- **The family at Crane Lake.** They give Walt's landing sign: a white stone on a stump. His sister Aili keeps
  unsigned Christmas cards from Ontario in a coffee can and will never say he is alive.

**The company and the press.** Northmark Metals, the new company, has the old company's men: Carl Brekke, who faked
the 1976 water report, and Wade Helstrom ("Clean Boots"), who ran the watchers. Decent people work there too: Dan in
community relations and Tasha the geologist. Northmark is building its Kettle project on that faked water baseline.
The watching escalates: a green Kevlar with no rods, then the player's camp searched and folded neater than they left
it, then the canoe found adrift at dawn with the paddle gone. Tauno calls Maggie Tomczak, a Duluth journalist, who
sets the meeting point.

**The far end (Act III).** The player pieces it together from the rods (205, then 50), the wrong hill the company dug
on Pietro, the notch behind the creek mouth, and the white stone on the stump at the dam. That leads to Gull's dead-end
east bay. An unmapped carry runs up the beaver flowage to the camp Walt built from the 1976 fly camp's leftovers. Under
a floorboard are three pages he tore out. From there, 620 m toward the midwinter sunrise, is the old test pit: the
cellar. In it are two ammunition cans of papers (field books, the pencilled false report, a bribe ledger, a threat
memo) and Eino's note on top: *W. — Saturday. Bring the good coffee.*

**The run.** Picking up the note starts it. The player paddles all night north to Lake One, Walt's own escape route,
with no campsites and the light on or off, hiding in reeds. A scanner picks up the watchers. Being caught is never an
ending: the player goes back to the last checkpoint, still holding the cans.

**The endings.** At dawn in the lot, Maggie's wagon is at one end and Dan's truck, with Brekke in it, at the other. The
player has to choose one of two:
- **The journalist.** The men fall, the mine faces a fair fight, and the player chooses whether to tell her Walt is
  alive.
- **The company's money.** $250,000, paid as a leash. The project is built on the lie, and Walt's friends turn away.

There is no "caught" ending and no "put it back" ending. Whichever ending happens, the game goes on, and some weeks
later Josie hands over an unsigned postcard from Atikokan. Its only signature is a small drawing of a stone on a stump.

Never told: who killed Eino. Never shown: Walt.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Co-op and the story.** Does a friend in the session run the cellar night with you, in two canoes with twice the
   silhouette, or is the story the host's alone? The files assume it is solo and the host's own save.
2. **The camp after the journalist ending.** May the player have the ranger haul Walt's camp out, or does it always
   stay?
3. **The money ending's cold shoulder.** The keepers who liked Walt drop their board tasks for good. Should one of
   them soften in a later year?

**Settled.** 2026-11-05: only two endings, the journalist or the company's money. The bible's third ending, "both
hands" (photos to the journalist, papers to the company), is cut. Variants inside the journalist ending (Walt kept out
or named) stay.
