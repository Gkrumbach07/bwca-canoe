## 1. How a search for an overdue or missing canoeist ran (1960s–80s, and how it runs now)

### Takeaway
On the Minnesota side a search is a county sheriff's job. In St. Louis County (the Ely side) it is backed by a volunteer Sheriff's Rescue Squad, founded in 1958. Lake and Cook counties cover the other entry areas. Forest Service, DNR, State Patrol and National Guard aircraft join larger searches. On the water the squad's main job has long been "fatality recovery" by dragging and diving. I found no primary account of a specific 1960s–80s BWCA search, so the period procedure below is pieced together from how the agencies describe themselves and from later cases.

### Cited Findings
- The St. Louis County Volunteer Rescue Squad was founded on August 23, 1958, by three men who approached the sheriff. The county attorney said state legislation was needed before the county could accept such a unit. — [SLCRS About Us](https://www.slcrs.org/about-us)
- The squad's wilderness searches cover "lost or missing hunters, hikers, berry-pickers, campers, canoeists, and downed aircraft". It also helps deputies find evidence "such as weapons and stolen property". — [SLCRS About Us](https://www.slcrs.org/about-us)
- On the water: "The primary activity on the water is fatality recovery, using a combination of dragging and diving techniques". Underwater cameras, side-scan sonar and cadaver dogs were added only "in recent years". So in the 1960s–80s, recovery would have meant grappling hooks or drag lines and divers. — [SLCRS About Us](https://www.slcrs.org/about-us)
- The squad is "often the primary agency in capsizings, strandings, and medical emergencies in areas which are accessible only by boat". Each spring and summer its members inspect the rental boats at resorts and outfitters. They also help deputies investigate water accidents. — [SLCRS About Us](https://www.slcrs.org/about-us)
- A modern overdue-party search (2017), useful for its shape:
  - A pair of experienced trippers missed a portage on the way out and got deep into a swamp. They lived on one packet of oatmeal each a day.
  - Coworkers and family reported them when they did not return on their exit day. The search began three days later and found them the next night, when they signalled search aircraft with a flashlight.
  - An Air National Guard Black Hawk lifted them out, and they were taken to Ely hospital.
  - Agencies involved: St. Louis County Rescue Squad, State Patrol, National Guard, Forest Service, DNR.
  - The squad captain: "It was a phenomenal search — one of the best I've been on". — [Quetico Superior Wilderness News, 2017](https://queticosuperior.org/lost-canoeists-rescued-getting-stranded-boundary-waters/)
- A modern recovery, as a pattern:
  - Passing canoeists found a capsized canoe and a partly submerged body on Lake Agnes (Quetico side) and called 911.
  - "Several officials" recovered the body, and the St. Louis County Sheriff's Office investigated.
  - A life jacket was found at the scene but was not being worn. The person had been on a solo trip. — [WCCO/CBS Minnesota, May 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/officials-find-capsize-canoe-submerged-body-on-lake-agnes/)
- The Forest Service's own account of its limits (2026): the wilderness is "too large and remote to safely locate and escort every visitor". Emergency response there is "challenging even on a normal day". — [Quetico Superior Wilderness News, July 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/forest-service-to-close-the-entire-bwcaw-as-wildfires-spread/)
- The Truman 1949 airspace order still allowed low flight "Conducting rescue operations", "Making an emergency landing", and on official business of the U.S., the State, or Cook, St. Louis and Lake counties. So search aircraft were always exempt. — [Executive Order 10092, Truman Library](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/executive-orders/10092/executive-order-10092)
- The U.S. Forest Service floatplane base in Ely has had DHC-2 Beavers since 1956 (first Beaver arrived that year). Today three of the Forest Service's Beavers are based there. They can drop about 1,000 lb of water from a 125-gallon tank, and they spot new fires. — [Quetico Superior Wilderness News, Sept 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/beaver-floatplanes-carried-idaho-hotshots-into-boundary-waters-to-fight-bear-trap-fire/)
- In the 1999 blowdown rescue, "Pilots from different agencies systematically searched campsites, portages, and bodies of water", and "Outfitters and guides took to motorized boats, searching lakes, and evacuating the injured". This is the community-response pattern: local outfitters and guides as de facto rescuers. — [QSWN review of *Gunflint Falling*](https://queticosuperior.org/book-review-gunflint-falling-by-cary-j-griffith/)

### Inferences
- A period-true 1970s search would likely run like this:
  - The party is reported overdue by family or the outfitter, often a day or more after the exit date.
  - The sheriff calls out the volunteer rescue squad.
  - Forest Service Beavers and private floatplane pilots fly the route.
  - Outfitters and resort men search in motorboats where motors were still allowed.
  - If a canoe turns up swamped, the work shifts from search to "recovery", with dragging and divers.
- "Presumed drowned" in fiction would plausibly rest on three things: an overturned or swamped canoe found, paddles or packs washed up, and an unrecovered body in deep, cold water. That is my inference; no source states the legal standard.
- The pre-sonar recovery era (dragging by hand, divers working blind in tea-coloured water) is a strong, true-feeling texture for the 1970s.

### Gaps
- No dated 1960s–80s BWCA search account was found with its duration, number of searchers, or how it ended. The newspaper archives that would hold them (Ely Echo, Ely Miner, Cook County News-Herald, Duluth News Tribune microfilm, Minnesota Digital Newspaper Hub) were not reachable by text search.
- Civil Air Patrol involvement in BWCA searches is plausible but unconfirmed. No source was found.
- No source was found on the Ontario side (OPP, MNR and Quetico rangers) or on how cross-border searches were coordinated.
- No source was found on how a missing person is legally declared dead in Minnesota (the time period, a court petition). This needs a legal source.
- No source was found on Lake County or Cook County rescue squad founding dates.

## 2. Floatplanes, bush pilots and the 1949 airban

### Takeaway
Before 1949, resorts and private cabins inside the roadless areas were reached by floatplane. Truman's Executive Order 10092 (Dec 17, 1949) closed the airspace below 4,000 ft above sea level over the roadless areas. It came into force after Jan 1, 1951, with exemptions for emergencies, rescue and official business. The Forest Service's Ely Beavers (from 1956) became the lasting "official" floatplanes. Private fly-ins moved to the Canadian side, for example via Lac La Croix and the Sand Point Lake customs landing.

### Cited Findings
- EO 10092 reserves "The airspace below the altitude of 4,000 feet above sea level" over the Superior, Little Indian Sioux and Caribou Roadless Areas in Cook, Lake and St. Louis counties. "After January 1, 1951, no person shall navigate an aircraft within this airspace reservation except…" as permitted. — [Truman Library, EO 10092](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/executive-orders/10092/executive-order-10092)
- The order had a grace period for flights to private land where flying was "a customary means of ingress". The Truman Library transcription gives the end date as "until January 1, 1942". That is impossible for a 1949 order and is almost certainly a typo, probably for 1952. Treat it as unverified. — [Truman Library, EO 10092](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/executive-orders/10092/executive-order-10092)
- The order was "In response to airplane landings and overflights that threatened the canoe country". — [Save the Boundary Waters, history timeline](https://www.savetheboundarywaters.org/updates/boundary-waters-day-history-protecting-wilderness)
- In 1948 the Thye-Blatnik Act "authorized the government to purchase the few remaining privately owned homes and resorts within the area". — [Wikipedia, BWCAW (raw)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness)
- What the airban did to one inholder, Dorothy Molter on Knife Lake: after the order, "all of Dorothy's supplies had to be hauled in over fifteen miles of lakes and overland portages from the nearest road at Moose Lake". A group of locals called "Dorothy's Angels" brought her mail and supplies. She could no longer bring in bottled pop, so she began brewing root beer. — [MNopedia, Dorothy Molter](https://www.mnopedia.org/person/molter-dorothy-1907-1986)
- "bush planes" were used by trappers and prospectors in Minnesota "as early as the 1920s". The Beaver (de Havilland Canada, just after WWII) has two large doors for fast loading and runs on wheels, floats or skis. A pilot at the Ely Seaplane Base called it "the Swiss Army knife of bush planes". — [QSWN, Sept 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/beaver-floatplanes-carried-idaho-hotshots-into-boundary-waters-to-fight-bear-trap-fire/)
- Fire crews are still flown in by Beaver: "An additional crew of six has been ordered from Ely and will arrive via Beaver Float plane" for a 2018 fire on Ogishkemuncie. — [Boundary Waters Blog, 2018](https://www.boundarywatersblog.com/small-fire-in-the-boundary-waters/)
- Canadian-side fly-ins: Quetico's entry points from Lac La Croix are reached by floatplane. Because "a landing is made at the Canadian Customs Station on Sand Point Lake (enroute), no advance customs clearance is necessary". — [Boundary Waters Outfitters (Ely) FAQ](https://boundarywaters.com/faq-items/quetico-provincial-park-differ-bwca/)

### Inferences
- A 1970s Ely or Grand Marais character could plausibly be a floatplane pilot who flew resort guests in before 1951 and now flies fishermen to Canadian outposts. The airban would be a still-sore local grievance.
- A low plane over the wilderness in the 1970s is noticeable and suspicious unless it is Forest Service or rescue. That is a good plot lever.

### Gaps
- No named Ely or Grand Marais floatplane operators from the 1950s–70s were confirmed. The private seaplane bases on Shagawa Lake and Burntside were not researched.
- Forest Service fire-patrol methods of the 1960s–70s (aerial detection flights versus lookout towers) were not documented.
- Whether Forest Service Otters (DHC-3) were based in Ely as well as Beavers is unconfirmed.

## 3. Rangers, ranger stations, the border crossing, and Vietnam-era draft resisters

### Takeaway
The border is a shared water route under the 1842 Webster–Ashburton Treaty. It is marked by small metal reference markers set in rock, installed from 1908. Paddlers crossing into Quetico from the Ely side funnel through Prairie Portage, where they pay fees and buy Ontario fishing licences. Others enter by other routes, such as Cache Bay off Saganaga or the Sand Point customs landing for fly-ins. Canada's Remote Area Border Crossing (RABC) permit is being phased out: it ends Sept 14, 2026, and is replaced by telephone reporting sites. I found no documented case of a Vietnam-era draft resister crossing to Canada through the BWCA or Quetico.

### Cited Findings
- The Webster–Ashburton Treaty (1842): "all the usual portages along the line from Lake Superior to the Lake of the Woods… shall be free and open to the use of the citizens and subjects of both countries". — [QSWN, "How is the international border marked"](https://queticosuperior.org/how-is-the-international-border-marked-in-the-bwca/)
- Border marking:
  - Most of the border had been surveyed by 1874. "Crews began installing metal markers starting in 1908" (an International Boundary Commission technician).
  - Since 1925 the IBC has been the permanent agency maintaining the line. It keeps a cleared swath 10 ft either side of the boundary on land.
  - The markers are "Short metal reference markers… punched firmly into hard granite rock". — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/how-is-the-international-border-marked-in-the-bwca/)
- Prairie Portage:
  - "All paddle-in canoe trips going into Quetico Park from the Ely area funnel through Prairie Portage. There are 15 parties daily."
  - "At 'Prairie' you will pay your camping fees, and purchase your non-resident Ontario fishing licenses."
  - It is reached by "a 6-mile paddle up the Moose Lake Chain". "Most parties reach the portage about lunch time." — [Boundary Waters Outfitters FAQ](https://boundarywaters.com/faq-items/quetico-provincial-park-differ-bwca/)
- Quetico portages and campsites are "a little more rugged" than the U.S. side, with no portage crews. Almost all live bait is banned there. — [same FAQ](https://boundarywaters.com/faq-items/quetico-provincial-park-differ-bwca/)
- The RABC permit let visitors "enter Canada at remote locations… without reporting to a staffed customs office". The program was paused in September 2024. Per the CBSA, "Effective Sept 14, 2026, the Remote Area Border Crossing (RABC) program will be discontinued", replaced by reporting at "designated telephone reporting site[s]". Returning to the U.S. means reporting to CBP in person or by app. — [QSWN, Dec 2025](https://queticosuperior.org/remote-area-border-crossing-permits-on-pause-for-quetico/)
- An RABC permit was applied for by mail or email with two pieces of ID and was valid for the calendar year. — [Soka Outdoors](https://www.sokaoutdoors.com/articles/crossing-borders-boundary-waters-to-quetico-part-one)
- A remembered border scene, May 1980, from a bwca.com poster:
  - A U.S. fighter jet passed perhaps 150 ft over Saganaga while they paddled "from the old Canadian border stop toward Cache Bay".
  - They drank straight from the lake with "tin Sierra cups (no filters in those days)".
  - This also shows a staffed Canadian border stop on Saganaga in 1980. — [bwca.com forum, "strange things" thread](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1353366&forumID=12&confID=1)
- Quetico's history: when the park was established in the early 20th century "it displaced Anishinaabe families and restricted their traditional land use". The name comes from the Ojibwe "Gwetaming". Lac La Croix First Nation leaders call Quetico Lake sacred and "occupied by living spirits". — [QSWN, Feb 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/a-snapshot-of-quetico-provincial-parks-past/)
- Wilderness rangers today work under the "Pagami protocol" (see §5). Under it they must work under someone with certain operational qualifications and check in "day and night". — [MPR News, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2021/09/10/ten-years-later-lessons-from-the-pagami-creek-fire)
- Draft resisters: searches of Quetico Superior Wilderness News for "Vietnam" and "draft dodger" found only a veterans' trip story. Google searches returned nothing relevant. — [QSWN search result](https://queticosuperior.org/honoring-veterans-boundary-waters-wilderness-experience/)

### Inferences
- Paddling into Canada through the wilderness was, legally, a reporting problem rather than a sealed border. In the 1960s–70s the in-person stations (Prairie Portage, Saganaga, Lac La Croix and Sand Point area) were the checkpoints. A fictional draft resister going "out through the woods" would be plausible, but it is invented. No documented case was found, so it should be treated as fiction rather than history.
- The 2026 end of RABC is a fresh, real "the border just changed" detail for any present-day frame.

### Gaps
- **No documented case of a Vietnam-era draft resister crossing via the BWCA or Quetico was found.** Wikipedia's draft-evasion article has no Minnesota or canoe-route mention. Most documented routes were by road and air to Toronto, Montreal and Vancouver.
- The history of the Kawishiwi and Gunflint ranger stations, and of wilderness-ranger work in the 1960s–70s (latrine and fire-grate crews, permit checks, "can and bottle ban" enforcement), was not found.
- When the Prairie Portage Canadian ranger and customs station began, and when the Saganaga customs stop closed, are both unknown.
- The pre-RABC history (CANPASS-Remote Area, earlier systems) is unknown.

## 4. What remains of the past inside the wilderness today

### Takeaway
The traces are ancient quarries (Knife Lake siltstone, possibly 11,000–12,000 years old, found in 2009 in ground bared by prescribed burns), inholder sites (Dorothy Molter's Isle of Pines), logging-era routes (Four Mile Portage), old mine pits (a gold-mine site near the Kelly–Jack lakes portage), border markers, and the junk that forum veterans remember finding. Specific lilacs, rhubarb and foundation stories were not confirmed in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- **Knife Lake quarry (handle with care):**
  - Researchers have studied "ancient quarries on Knife Lake". Tests suggest humans "could have been here 11,000 to 12,000 years ago", possibly the oldest such site in Minnesota.
  - The quarries were found in 2009 by a Superior National Forest team "Searching areas recently exposed by prescribed burns". People there shaped siltstone with antler and granite into "knives, scrapers, spear points and adzes".
  - Soil samples were taken at night under red light only. — [QSWN, 2013](https://queticosuperior.org/archaeologist-says-knife-lake-site-could-be-earliest-minnesota-settlement/)
  - Fire as the revealer is a strong, true story hinge: burns expose the past. — [QSWN, "Reconstructing the Past"](https://queticosuperior.org/reconstructing-the-past/)
- **Dorothy Molter:**
  - A nurse who first came to Bill Berglund's Isle of Pines resort on Knife Lake in 1930 and lived and worked there from 1934. Berglund taught her to portage, run dogs and cut ice.
  - Outfitters marked Isle of Pines on canoe maps "as a place to stop for medical help", which earned her the nickname "Nightingale of the Northwoods".
  - Berglund died in 1948, and she refused to sell to the Forest Service.
  - She brewed root beer "From the 1950s through the mid-1980s". — [MNopedia](https://www.mnopedia.org/person/molter-dorothy-1907-1986)
  - She lived on the Isle of Pines for 56 years and was "the last year-round resident of the BWCA". — [QSWN, "Magical history tour"](https://queticosuperior.org/magical-history-tour-through-the-bwca/)
- **Logging traces:**
  - "The Swallow and Hopkins logging company is said to have hauled 350 million board feet of pine over the Four Mile Portage over 10 years." — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/magical-history-tour-through-the-bwca/)
  - Around the Pagami Creek area, "as late as the 1970s they were still pulling timber out of some of this country" (a Forest Service fire behaviour analyst). — [QSWN, 2012](https://queticosuperior.org/1999-blowdown-forest-management-practices-impacts-pagami-creek-fire/)
- **Old gold mine:** "On the north end of the Kelly to Jack portage there is a short trail that leads to an old abandoned gold mine and lots of artifacts lying around." — [bwca.com forum, 2023](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1310948&forumID=16&confID=1&vt=1674751687)
- **Primitive Management Areas:** here the Forest Service "has removed campsite features and abandoned old portage trails". Expect "deadfall, dense brush, beaver dams, and mud". These are ghost portages a character could find. — [QSWN, 2025](https://queticosuperior.org/exploring-the-primitive-management-areas-of-the-bwcaw/)
- **"Strange finds" from bwca.com members** (first-person, unverified):
  - "A newly placed headstone and a half empty bottle of Jack Danials cemented into the the ground overlooking Pipestone".
  - A rusted 1940s-style car "Many miles from any known roads" NW of Silver Bay (Superior National Forest, not the BWCA).
  - A film canister with a tab of what looked like LSD at a campsite on the Island River.
  - Foxfire glowing from a wet log, taken for "a lost soul" at Iron Lake campground in 1976.
  - All from the [bwca.com "strange things" thread](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1353366&forumID=12&confID=1)
- **Things fished up** (bwca.com members):
  - "A garden round point shovel from 30 ft of water at the mouth where Curtain Falls flows into Iron Lake."
  - A rod lost the previous year was hooked back up on the first cast.
  - A lost chain stringer of walleye was snagged back off the bottom with a treble-hook Daredevle. — [bwca.com "strangest catch" thread](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1321163&forumID=14&confID=1&vt=1722411383)
- **Remembered 1999 aftermath:**
  - A chainsaw left hanging where its chain had pinched in a tree over a portage.
  - A whole red pine "torn off its ledge rock, roots and all… floating down Basswood Lake".
  - A campsite with "the daily newspaper drying" on lines. — [QuietJourney forum](https://quietjourney.com/community/YABB.cgi?num=1284168482)

### Inferences
- Burns and blowdowns as revealers (quarries found after prescribed burns, ground bared after fire) is a documented, believable way for an old object to surface decades later.
- A rusted shovel or tools at 30 ft below a falls is a believable "found" object.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: specific resort foundations, lilacs or rhubarb at old resort sites inside the BWCAW (e.g., on Basswood, Crooked or Knife), logging dams (e.g., on the Kawishiwi or Isabella), rail grades, fire-tower locations, and old Forest Service portage signs. These are commonly reported but no source was retrieved.
- Mining test pits and 1970s exploration drill sites (INCO on the Spruce Road, near Ely): no first-hand description of a 1970s core shed or drill site was found. The DNR drill core library page returned 404. A UMN drill-hole database exists (Patelke 2003, conservancy.umn.edu) but was not readable. See the sibling note `mining_copper_nickel.md` for mining history.
- Kekekabic Trail history: only that volunteers are pushing it "closer to Ely, one mile at a time" (QSWN 2026 title). Its 1930s fire-trail origin is unverified.
- Benny Ambrose (Ottertrack Lake inholder): no text retrieved.

## 5. The big events and how people remember them

### Takeaway
Each event has a signature image:
- The **1999 derecho**: a sky "line" over Ely and a freight-train roar, then 20 million trees down and two weeks to get everyone out.
- **Cavity Lake (2006)** and **Ham Lake (2007)**: fires in the blowdown fuels. Ham Lake was started by a camper and burned 75,000+ acres and 61 homes on the Gunflint.
- **Pagami Creek (2011)**: a 16-mile run in one day, a plume seen from space, and six wilderness rangers sheltering on Insula.
- **Greenwood (2021)**: the first whole-BWCA closure since the 1970s.
- **2026**: a second full closure, from July 14, during the "July Lightning Incident" (Bear Trap, Thumb, Sioux and Little Knife fires).

### Cited Findings

**1999 blowdown (Boundary Waters–Canadian derecho, July 4, 1999)**
- Size is reported variously. About 20 million trees fell in the BWCA. The swath was about 30 miles long and 10 miles wide, about 500,000 acres. The NWS figure is "478,000 acres of forest, in a swath 12 miles by 30 miles". Downed trunks were "blown into drifts 15 to 20 feet high". — [QSWN, 2024](https://queticosuperior.org/fourth-of-july-marks-20th-anniversary-of-boundary-waters-blowdown/)
  - Contradicted by Wikipedia's "about 370,000 acres within the BWCAW". — [Wikipedia, BWCAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness)
  - A forum summary gives 477,000 acres. — [QuietJourney](https://quietjourney.com/community/YABB.cgi?num=1284168482)
- Casualties: "two people in Canada" died, "there were no deaths in the Boundary Waters". It took "nearly two weeks for all people who had been in the wilderness to safely get out", and 60 were injured. — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/fourth-of-july-marks-20th-anniversary-of-boundary-waters-blowdown/)
  - Contradicted by a forum summary saying 60 were injured in the BWCAW and "one person was killed when the victim drowned". — [QuietJourney](https://quietjourney.com/community/YABB.cgi?num=1284168482)
  - Flag: deaths inside the BWCAW are unresolved.
- Peak winds were believed to be about 90 mph (QSWN/NOAA). Wikipedia says up to 100 mph. — same sources as above.
- Remembered voices (forum, first person):
  - From Ely, "a solid sweep of line separating a white gray sky from a deep gray blue sky", then "a wall of wind and dust blew up Sheridan… setting off car alarms". A driveway was stacked with jack pines "two and three high" and took four hours of chainsaw work to clear.
  - On Kawnipi, they tied the canoe to trees and hid behind rocks; "the water in Kawnipi was blowing up out of the lake… it sounded like a frieght train".
  - A guide sheltered his crew on a rocky portage "as the trees started crashing down", then sat through an hour of near-constant lightning that night.
  - A solo paddler on the Basswood River saw the second storm's lightning together with the Ely fireworks.
  - All from [QuietJourney thread](https://quietjourney.com/community/YABB.cgi?num=1284168482)
- From the book *Gunflint Falling* (Cary J. Griffith):
  - Witnesses saw trees "being lifted up and out of the ground and set back down".
  - Three women were injured when a tree flattened their tent.
  - Pilots from several agencies searched campsites and portages systematically. — [QSWN review](https://queticosuperior.org/book-review-gunflint-falling-by-cary-j-griffith/)

**Cavity Lake (2006) and Ham Lake (2007)**
- The blowdown fuels "fuel[ed] the 31,830 acre Cavity Lake Fire in 2006 and the 36,443 acre Ham Lake Fire in 2007". These are U.S.-side acres. — [QSWN, 2012](https://queticosuperior.org/1999-blowdown-forest-management-practices-impacts-pagami-creek-fire/)
- Ham Lake facts:
  - Reported at 11:44 a.m. on May 5, 2007.
  - It burned 75,484 acres on both sides of the border and destroyed 61 residences, 17 commercial structures and 55 outbuildings.
  - Suppression cost $10.9 million. At its peak, 1,091 firefighters, 13 helicopters and 8 bulldozers were on it.
  - It was contained May 19 after rain of 0.1–0.5 in on May 14. — [Historical marker text via ExploreHere/HMDB](https://explorehere.app/markers/kh77abhxpqnr9nag9qchmkdcvh7fevec/the-2007-ham-lake-fire)
- The cause was "a campsite just outside of the Boundary Waters Canoe Area". A mandatory evacuation was ordered for the west end of the Gunflint Trail. — [MPR News, May 7, 2007](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2007/05/07/fire)
- A first-person account from a Gunflint outfitter and volunteer firefighter:
  - Smoke was smelled "a little after 11:00" at Tuscarora Lodge on Round Lake.
  - Volunteer firefighters took the truck out and saw "flames jumped from tree top to tree top over Round Lake Road". A few minutes later they would have been cut off.
  - Aircraft made "drop after drop of fire retardant, foam, and water", and sprinkler systems went onto buildings. — [Boundary Waters Blog, 2017 repost of 2007 entries](https://www.boundarywatersblog.com/ham-lake-fire-ten-year-anniversary/)
- The day after, in the same author's words: "The sound of trees crackling and burning, the roar of the fire, and the occasional propane tank exploding are sounds I can't get out of my head." Two weeks later it was 37°F with a cold rain turning to snow. — [Boundary Waters Blog](https://www.boundarywatersblog.com/ham-fire-may-19th-2007-part-1/)
- Season matters: Cavity Lake burned in late summer and Ham Lake in early May, which affects how the land recovered. — [QSWN Fire FAQs, 2021](https://queticosuperior.org/fire-faqs-critical-context-for-canoe-country-combustion/)

**Pagami Creek (2011)**
- Lightning start, Aug 18, 2011. It reached 92,682 acres. Smoke drifted to the Upper Peninsula, Ontario and Chicago. It was the largest natural wildfire in Minnesota in more than a century. — [Wikipedia (raw)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagami_Creek_Fire)
- "Unprecedented weather conditions drove the fire 16 miles on Sept. 12, 2011". The fire "overtook some campers and wilderness rangers, who only survived by deploying fire shelters and jumping in lakes". About 40 campsites were closed for years. — [MPR News, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2021/09/10/ten-years-later-lessons-from-the-pagami-creek-fire)
- The rangers' entrapment, from a Forest Service video:
  - Six wilderness rangers sent to clear visitors from Lake Three, Hudson and Insula ran for open water by canoe.
  - On Insula they met 40–50 mph winds and three-foot swells. Four deployed shelters on "a tiny barren island". Two deployed shelters in the lake and became hypothermic.
  - Afterward came rain and hail. They camped on the island and were flown out the next day.
  - They "recall frustration with their superiors" and say their earlier advice to evacuate was ignored. — [QSWN, 2016](https://queticosuperior.org/video-tells-riveting-story-of-six-rangers-caught-by-pagami-creek-fire/)
- The zone fire manager on the aftermath: "Nobody meant to lose track of those rangers in that fire." This led to the "Pagami protocol" and to earlier, bigger closures. — [MPR News, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2021/09/10/ten-years-later-lessons-from-the-pagami-creek-fire)
- Before Pagami, "none of the 435 natural fires that burned in the BWCAW since 1987 grew beyond the wilderness boundaries". About 50 small fires in its footprint had been put out over the previous decades. — [QSWN, 2012](https://queticosuperior.org/1999-blowdown-forest-management-practices-impacts-pagami-creek-fire/); [MPR, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2021/09/10/ten-years-later-lessons-from-the-pagami-creek-fire)

**Greenwood (2021) and the full closure**
- First noted Aug 15, 2021 near Greenwood Lake (Lake County), believed to be lightning. It caused the evacuation of more than 290 homes and "the temporary closure of the entire nearby Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness for the first time since the 1970s". — [Wikipedia, Greenwood Fire (raw)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_Fire)
- About 26,000 acres, the largest in 10 years. Historical comparison: in 1863–64, 445,000 acres burned, "almost half of the current BWCAW". — [QSWN Fire FAQs](https://queticosuperior.org/fire-faqs-critical-context-for-canoe-country-combustion/)

**2026 (very recent; verify before using)**
- From 12:01 a.m. July 14, 2026, the whole BWCAW closed. Up to 19 fires had started on the forest since July 7. The closure order ran "through October 1, 2026" as an outer limit. — [QSWN tracker](https://queticosuperior.org/forest-service-to-close-the-entire-bwcaw-as-wildfires-spread/)
- Final footprint of the "July Lightning Incident": 71,675 acres (62,652 U.S., 9,023 Canada), from the Sioux (6,833), Bear Trap (39,327) and Thumb (25,515) fires and others. About 150 of roughly 2,000 campsites showed fire effects. Rangers patrolled by canoe on Loon Lake. — [QSWN, Sept 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/seven-wildfires-burning-in-the-superior-national-forest-and-bwca-after-lightning-storms/)
- On July 29, Forest Service Beavers flew a 21-person Idaho hotshot crew from the Ely Seaplane Base to Gull Lake. — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/beaver-floatplanes-carried-idaho-hotshots-into-boundary-waters-to-fight-bear-trap-fire/)
- A paddler's account (Little Knife Fire, July 2026): on Little Saganaga, a "well-defined plume being carried directly northeast by strong winds" against a clear sky. — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/escaping-ahead-of-the-little-knife-fire-in-the-bwca/)

**Twin Metals and mining fights**
- 2026: Gov. Walz's Executive Order 26-10 halted nonferrous mining permitting and leases in the Rainy River Headwaters. On Sept 11 the DNR cancelled one Twin Metals state lease (MM-9455-N), effective Nov 10, 2026. A 2023 federal order had withdrawn 225,504 acres. Congress has moved to overturn the 20-year federal ban. — [QSWN, Sept 2026](https://queticosuperior.org/dnr-cancels-one-twin-metals-lease-further-protecting-boundary-waters-and-surrounding-watershed/)
- The 1978 BWCA Wilderness Act banned logging, mining and snowmobiles, phased out most motors (leaving 22% of the water open to them), and set up a 220,000-acre no-mining buffer. — [Save the Boundary Waters](https://www.savetheboundarywaters.org/updates/boundary-waters-day-history-protecting-wilderness)
- Details of the 2016 lease non-renewal were not retrieved here; see `mining_copper_nickel.md`.

### Inferences
- The people who live there remember fire through sound (trees crackling, propane tanks exploding), smell (smoke before sight), and the grief of "our way of life". A Gunflint character from 2007 would carry those memories.
- A Pagami-style story (staff on the ground overruled by headquarters, a race by canoe to open water, sheltering on bare rock) is documented and could inspire fiction. Real rangers are not named here.

### Gaps
- Floods and drought years were not researched, for example the 1976 drought or the 2012 Duluth flood's effect on canoe country.
- Which 1970s event closed the whole BWCA (the Wikipedia claim "first time since the 1970s") is not identified. Possibly the 1976 Roy Lake / Little Sioux era fires; unverified.
- Cavity Lake ignition details (lightning, July 2006) and its evacuations were not retrieved.

## 6. 1970s material culture: gear, maps, food, canoes, notebooks

### Takeaway
The 1960s–70s outfit was an aluminum Grumman canoe from a big Ely outfitter (Canoe Country Outfitters was North America's largest Grumman dealer), canvas Duluth/Poirier packs, and lake water drunk unfiltered from Sierra cups. Most map, food and notebook specifics were not confirmed in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Ely's Canoe Country Outfitters (founded after 1946 by a Navy veteran and his wife) became "the largest canoe outfitter in the world and the largest Grumman canoe dealer in North America". Its fleet grew from 10 wood-canvas canoes to over 400. It was dubbed the "Canoe King of Ely" in 1960s *Argosy*. Only two other outfitters existed in Ely when it started. A photo inscription records "29 portages" in one day on a Quetico bushwhack. — [QSWN, 2007](https://queticosuperior.org/bill-rom-canoe-king-ely/)
- The Duluth pack ("Poirier pack") is heavy canvas and leather and "nearly square in order to fit easily in the bottom of a canoe". It is named for Camille Poirier, a 19th-century French-Canadian shoemaker in Duluth. — [Wikipedia, Duluth pack (raw)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth_pack)
- Drinking straight from the lake in 1980: "tin Sierra cups (no filters in those days)". — [bwca.com forum](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1353366&forumID=12&confID=1)
- Fishing culture: chain stringers of walleye hung over the side, slip bobbers with leeches, Daredevle spoons, Rapalas. Live bait was still allowed in Quetico in the remembered past and is now almost all banned. — [bwca.com "strangest catch" thread](https://bwca.com/index.cfm?fuseaction=forum.thread&threadId=1321163&forumID=14&confID=1&vt=1722411383); [Boundary Waters Outfitters FAQ](https://boundarywaters.com/faq-items/quetico-provincial-park-differ-bwca/)
- Map culture today: paddlers carry more than one map set. Voyageur Maps print history, geology and burn areas on the back. — [QSWN](https://queticosuperior.org/magical-history-tour-through-the-bwca/)
- Distance unit: portage lengths are given in rods (e.g., "the first 5-rod portage into Swamp Lake"). — [Boundary Waters Blog](https://www.boundarywatersblog.com/boundary-waters-loop-saganaga-knife-seagull/)

### Inferences
- A 1970s guide's notebook would plausibly log portages in rods, campsites by lake and point, walleye counts, and the outfitter's canoe number. The rod is the canonical BWCA unit, confirmed above.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed: Fisher Maps and McKenzie Maps (history, look, the 1970s scale and colour), 1970s trail food brands (e.g., Mountain House or Rich-Moor freeze-dried, Bisquick, Tang), the 1980s Forest Service "can and bottle ban", and what lake nicknames guides used. All are known-but-unsourced leads.
- No 1970s exploration drill site or core shed description was found (see §4 Gaps).
