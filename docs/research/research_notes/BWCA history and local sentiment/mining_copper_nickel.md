# Copper-nickel mining near the Boundary Waters: history and local sentiment (1948 – Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-03 (Election Day in Minnesota; governor's race results not known at time of writing).
Method note: WebSearch/WebFetch failed in this session (model error); all sources were fetched with curl (Bing News RSS for discovery, then the article pages). Wikipedia's API was rate-limited, but its HTML pages loaded. Quotes are kept short and copied from the fetched text.

## 1. Geology, discovery and early exploration (1948–1970s)

### Takeaway
The Duluth Complex is a 1.1-billion-year-old gabbro intrusion. Its northwestern base holds low-grade copper, nickel and platinum-group-metal sulfides. Serious interest began after a 1948 discovery. INCO got federal leases near Ely in 1966, and in the mid-1970s INCO (South Kawishiwi River, near Ely) and AMAX (test shaft near Babbitt, 1976) both explored hard. The state then paused everything for the 1974–79 Regional Copper-Nickel Study. Low copper prices and hard-to-process ore made both companies walk away. The core samples from that era are kept at the DNR Drill Core Library in Hibbing.

### Cited Findings
- The Duluth Complex formed about 1.1 billion years ago in the Midcontinent Rift. It is "one of the largest intrusions of gabbro on earth" and covers 4,715 km². Its lower portion along the northwestern margin holds "segregations of nickel, copper and platinum group elements," and attempts to mine them "are opposed by conservationists." — [Wikipedia: Duluth Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth_Complex)
- Most of the eastern Superior National Forest and the BWCAW sits on the Duluth Complex. The lakes lie in hollows eroded from the gabbro. — [Wikipedia: Duluth Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth_Complex)
- Copper-nickel was known in northern MN "since the 1800s," but serious interest came only after deposits were discovered in **1948** "near Duluth." Over the next two decades, exploration drilling found large low-grade deposits. — [MN Legislative Reference Library, Copper-Nickel issue guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- **1966**: the federal government granted INCO (International Nickel Co.) two preference-right mineral leases, **MNES-01352 and MNES-01353**, near the BWCAW in the Rainy River watershed. These leases "predate many of the environmental protection acts" of the late 1960s and 1970s. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- The INCO leases were negotiated with federal officials during the Lyndon Johnson administration. — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- 1969 and 1971: the Legislature funded University of Minnesota research on processing ("beneficiation") non-ferrous ore. Gov. Wendell Anderson then created an inter-agency task force, which issued a 1973 report on base-metal mining impacts. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- **1974**: AMAX Exploration and INCO "started exploring... in earnest." In May 1974 INCO asked the Forest Service for permits to mine federally leased land near Ely: "a large mine on the South Kawishiwi River just outside the BWCAW." Gov. Wendell Anderson opposed it because it was so close to the BWCAW (Minneapolis Tribune, 1974). — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- AMAX proposed a mine near Babbitt. After an MPCA hearing in July 1974 and "back and forth" with the Environmental Quality Council (EQC) and environmental groups, "AMAX was permitted to establish a test site in **1976**." Today's NorthMet/NewRange site is "in the same general region AMAX established a test shaft in 1976." — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Circle of Blue (Feb 2026) describes the copper-nickel minerals as "discovered in the region in the 1950s." Poor market conditions "deterred INCO from proceeding in the 1970s." A 1973 federal court ruling banned mining inside the wilderness itself. — [Circle of Blue, 2026-02-25](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/). (The LRL gives the discovery as 1948. The 1950s date may refer to later finds.)
- **Drill core library**: Minnesota law requires exploration companies to turn over a sample of their drill core. The cores are "generally two-inch thick rock cylinders" and are stored for good in the DNR library in Hibbing. In 2008 it held three two-story buildings "stuffed to the rafters with box upon cardboard box of rocks," with boxes "stacked seven deep on sturdy metal shelves." It held about 2.3 million feet of core, dating back to 1905, from 79 of 87 counties. The first library building went up in **1972**. — [Duluth News Tribune, 2008-02-10](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/news/drill-cores-are-treasure)
- The largest building is 200 ft × 80 ft × ~25 ft, with "narrow passageways" for a forklift between rows. Franconia Minerals used library cores to begin its Babbitt-area discovery (later part of Twin Metals). State geologist Harvey Thorleifson: "It's a treasure." — [DNT 2008](https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/news/drill-cores-are-treasure)
- In 2026 each core box holds about 10 ft of core and weighs about 20–25 lb. Laid end to end, the cores would reach "from International Falls to Chicago" (600+ miles). Supervisor Matt Carter thinks the collection is likely in North America's top five. It is open to the public. — [WDIO, 2026-01-23](https://www.wdio.com/lift-online/dnr-drill-core-library-is-unbelievable-resource/)
- The DNR is running a hyperspectral "CoreScan" imaging project on library core. It is non-destructive, so the samples stay available. — [MN DNR CoreScan project](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/lands_minerals/mpes_projects/dnr-corescan-project/index.html)
- An AP file photo caption describes "a prospecting drill rig bores into the bedrock near Ely, Minnesota, on Oct. 4, 2011, in search of copper, nickel and precious metals." This is a usable visual reference for a modern drill site. — [Finance & Commerce / AP, 2025-06](https://finance-commerce.com/2025/06/trump-copper-mining-boundary-waters-twin-metals/)
- Exploration plans today are framed as drilling "exploratory borings" at numbered drill sites, plus down-hole geophysics. Example: NewRange filed for up to 11 drill sites on school trust land 4.5 mi south of Babbitt on 2026-10-19. — [KQDS/Fox21, 2026-10-26](https://www.fox21online.com/news/local/mining-exploration-plan-near-babbitt-submitted-by-newrange/article_5fd046d6-4441-4843-9af4-6b103718a269.html)

### Inferences
- In the game, a 1970s INCO or AMAX core shed, or rusting drill-pad clearings in the Kawishiwi/Birch Lake area, would be historically plausible. The cores themselves would by law now be in Hibbing, not left in the woods.
- The same two areas (INCO near Ely/South Kawishiwi; AMAX near Babbitt) have been fought over twice, 50 years apart. That makes the story naturally generational: a 1970s parent and a 2020s child.

## 2. The 1974–79 Minnesota Regional Copper-Nickel Study

### Takeaway
After a 1974 EQC resolution, which in effect paused copper-nickel mining, the state spent five years and $4.3 million studying 2,100 square miles. The study concluded in 1979. It found that mining "could meet environmental standards if available technology was used wisely," valued the ore at about $50 billion, and projected 2,000+ jobs. Both sides still quote it.

### Cited Findings
- 1974: the EQC required "an adequate regional study" of environmental, social and economic impacts before accepting any EIS. This "effectively put a moratorium on copper-nickel mining in the region until the study's completion." — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Published 1979. Five years, $4.3 million, 180+ technical reports, 2,100 sq mi study area. Its goal was to assess region-wide effects and collect baseline data. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Findings: mining "could meet environment standards if available technology was used wisely," but production near existing iron facilities "might exceed established environmental guidelines." The study estimated **$50 billion** of minerals and **2,000+ new jobs**. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Aftermath: the public was "both excited by the economic possibilities... and concerned." Falling copper prices and difficult low-grade ore led AMAX and INCO to abandon their plans. The full set of reports is digitized at the LRL. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Circle of Blue (2026) calls it "a seminal 1979 state study" valuing the reserves at $50 billion and 2,000 jobs. — [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/)

### Inferences
- The study's two-sided conclusion (technically possible, risky near existing works, economically large) is a good neutral "document" an NPC could cite. Both sides can quote it.

### Gaps
- I did not read the study's technical volumes (water-quality and acid-drainage findings in detail).

## 3. The 1978 BWCA Wilderness Act and mineral protection

### Takeaway
The 1978 Act banned mining inside the wilderness. It created a ~222,000-acre Mining Protection Area along the road corridors, where the Secretary of Agriculture can acquire mineral rights. It did not cover the wider Rainy River headwaters. That gap is what the later 225,504-acre withdrawal tried to fill.

### Cited Findings
- Under the Act, "Mining became restricted in the BWCA and the Secretary of Agriculture has the authority to acquire mineral rights in the Wilderness and along three road corridors in a 222,000-acre (900 km²) Mining Protection Area." — [Wikipedia: BWCA Wilderness Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness_Act)
- A 1973 federal district court ruling banned mining within the wilderness. Carter signed the 1978 law, which expanded the area and set motor limits. — [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/)
- "Mining is not allowed inside the Boundary Waters, nor in a small buffer zone around it." — [MPR News, 2026-08-18](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/walz-executive-order-block-copper-mining-near-boundary-waters)
- Ely's resentment of the 1964/1978 laws carries into the mining fight. Bill Erzar: "It was devastating to this community." Former mayor Roger Skraba: "Is there a bad taste in my mouth? Absolutely. Will it go away? When I'm dead." — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine (citing press reports)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)

### Gaps
- Exact statutory section text (Pub. L. 95-495 §9 or similar) not fetched. I could not confirm whether "Mining Protection Area" is the statutory term or Wikipedia's wording.

## 4. Twin Metals and the federal leases: the back-and-forth (1966 – Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
The two 1966 INCO leases passed to Duluth Metals and then to Twin Metals Minnesota (formed 2010; fully owned by Antofagasta since 2014). They were renewed in 1989 and 2004. Since 2016 they have changed hands with each administration: not renewed (Obama, 2016), reinstated (Trump, 2018–19), cancelled (Biden, 2022), then the 20-year withdrawal (PLO 7917, Jan 2023). Congress overturned the withdrawal by Congressional Review Act (House Jan 2026, Senate 50–49 on 2026-04-16, signed 2026-04-27). Minnesota answered with a Walz executive order (2026-08-18) and a cancelled state lease (effective 2026-11-10). As of April 2026 the federal leases were still cancelled and in litigation. Interior would have to reissue them.

### Cited Findings (chronological)
- 1966 leases MNES-01352/-01353 to INCO; renewed 1989 and 2004. Twin Metals sought a third renewal in 2012. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- The leases passed to Duluth Metals, which partnered with Antofagasta to form Twin Metals Minnesota in 2010. In 2014 Antofagasta paid Duluth Metals $85 million for full ownership. — [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/)
- Twin Metals' operational HQ is in Ely; its corporate HQ is in St. Paul. Antofagasta is controlled by the Luksic family. — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- 2016-03-08: Interior's Solicitor said there was no obligation to renew. In June 2016 the Forest Service said it might withhold consent. The listening session in Duluth on 2016-07-14 drew about 500 people. The Ely meeting on 2016-07-20 drew about 900, with speakers split "about 50-50, with a narrow majority against." — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- December 2016: the Obama administration declined renewal. Forest Service Chief Tom Tidwell warned of "serious and irreparable harm" via Birch Lake and the Kawishiwi River. — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine). At the same time, 234,000 acres were temporarily withdrawn for two years pending a study. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- Late 2017: a new Interior Solicitor opinion (Daniel Jorjani) reversed the earlier one. A May 2018 BLM letter reinstated the 2004 leases. September 2018: the temporary withdrawal was lifted and the Forest Service study suspended (a redacted version was released in March 2020 after a Wilderness Society suit). **2019-05-15**: BLM formally renewed the leases. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine). Circle of Blue says "In 2017... Interior reinstated the leases." This conflicts with the LRL, AP and Wikipedia (2018 reinstatement, 2019 renewal). Use 2018/2019.
- June 2018: three lawsuits were filed. One came from Northeastern Minnesotans for Wilderness plus nine businesses, mostly outfitters. One came from The Wilderness Society, Center for Biological Diversity and Izaak Walton League. One came from Friends of the Boundary Waters. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- December 2019: Twin Metals submitted its mine plan. It is underground, with surface facilities near where the South Kawishiwi flows into Birch Lake, about 9 mi southeast of Ely. It would use dry-stack tailings, with about half backfilled underground. The plan claims 20,000 tons of ore per day from 400–4,500 ft deep, 25 years of operation, and 700 direct and 1,400 spin-off jobs. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine); [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/). A 2026 summary gives 1,156 acres, 25–30 years, about 750 jobs plus 1,500 indirect, and "$12 billion in revenue in the first 10 years" (company figures). — [Quetico Superior Wilderness News, 2026-10-24](https://queticosuperior.org/recent-poll-shows-most-minnesotans-oppose-new-mines-near-the-boundary-waters/)
- July 2019: Twin Metals dropped its tailings-pond plan for dry stacking. Chief regulatory officer Julie Padilla: "It removes any potential for failure of a dam." The DNR has questioned dry stack in Minnesota's wet, cold climate. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- November 2020: the DNR agreed with Northeastern Minnesotans for Wilderness (NMW) to consider changing its non-ferrous siting rules, possibly including a Rainy River watershed ban. In June 2020 NMW had sued under the Minnesota Environmental Rights Act. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- 2021-10-20: Biden's Forest Service applied for a 20-year withdrawal of about 225,000 acres. **2022-01-26**: Interior cancelled the two leases. Principal Deputy Solicitor Ann Marie Bledsoe Downes found the 2019 renewals violated "multiple legal authorities." February 2022: the MN DNR stopped its environmental review. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine); [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- **2023-01-26**: Interior Secretary Deb Haaland issued **Public Land Order 7917**, closing 225,504 acres of the Superior National Forest in the Rainy River watershed to mineral and geothermal leasing for 20 years. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine); [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/); [Quetico Superior News](https://queticosuperior.org/recent-poll-shows-most-minnesotans-oppose-new-mines-near-the-boundary-waters/) (acreage)
- June 2025: Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins and Interior Secretary Doug Burgum announced they would undo the withdrawal. Rollins: "the withdrawal was never needed." Trump had promised in St. Cloud in 2024 to reverse the moratorium. — [AP via Finance & Commerce, 2025-06](https://finance-commerce.com/2025/06/trump-copper-mining-boundary-waters-twin-metals/)
- June 2025: a provision to allow mining near the Boundary Waters was stripped from the budget bill (MPR, 2025-06-11, as listed by the LRL). In July/August 2025 a separate appropriations bill provision would have reinstated the leases. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel); [Twin Cities Pioneer Press, 2025-08-02 (via Bing listing)](https://www.twincities.com/2025/08/02/twin-metals-mine-bountary-waters/)
- November 2025 – January 2026: Franconia Minerals (a Twin Metals subsidiary) won DNR approval (Dec. 29, 2025 letter) to drill **19 exploratory boreholes** north and south of Birch Lake, with added conditions. DNR's Joseph Henderson said the company had "the right to explore for minerals on these leased properties." — [Duluth News Tribune via Inforum, 2026-01-02](https://www.inforum.com/news/minnesota/dnr-oks-exploratory-drilling-near-boundary-waters-area)
- **January 2026**: the House passed Rep. Pete Stauber's **H.J.Res.140** (CRA). Rep. Stauber had earlier filed H.R.978 in 2025. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/congress-overturns-bidens-boundary-waters-mining-ban/)
- **2026-04-16**: the Senate passed it **50–49**. Republicans Thom Tillis and Susan Collins voted no. E&E reports this is "the first time Congress has passed a CRA to rescind an order withdrawing certain public lands" from mining. — [E&E News, 2026-04-16](https://www.eenews.net/articles/congress-overturns-bidens-boundary-waters-mining-ban/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/16/minnesota-boundary-waters-mining-congress.html)
- **2026-04-27**: Trump signed it. Twin Metals paid $380,000 to the Bernhardt Group (led by former Interior Secretary David Bernhardt) in the year before. — [MinnPost Glean, 2026-04-30, citing Star Tribune](https://www.minnpost.com/glean/2026/04/twin-metals-minnesota-lobby-boundary-waters-policy-win/); [E&E (signing)](https://www.eenews.net/articles/trump-signs-resolution-favoring-mining-near-boundary-waters/)
- After the CRA: "The company's federal mineral leases were canceled under the Biden administration in 2022 and currently are tied up in litigation. The Interior Department would need to reissue those leases." — [E&E News, 2026-04-16](https://www.eenews.net/articles/congress-overturns-bidens-boundary-waters-mining-ban/)
- April 2026: in a decision Twin Metals calls an "April 2026 legal decision" by the DNR, the agency found existing state rules strong enough to protect the BWCAW. An administrative law judge reviewed it. — [Twin Metals statement in CBS/WCCO, 2026-08-19](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/boundary-water-mining-debate/). Mining Minnesota says that finding "is being reviewed in state court." — [MPR, 2026-08-18](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/walz-executive-order-block-copper-mining-near-boundary-waters)
- **2026-08-18**: Gov. Walz's executive order. It directs the DNR and MPCA not to issue nonferrous mining permits or leases, or do environmental review, in about 350 sq mi of Rainy River headwaters, and not to cooperate with federal review. It holds until NMW v. DNR litigation and rulemaking are resolved. It asks the DNR to draft a permanent-ban bill. — [MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/walz-executive-order-block-copper-mining-near-boundary-waters); [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- **2026-09-12/14**: the DNR cancelled one Franconia/Twin Metals **state** lease (about 242 acres of school trust land east of Babbitt near Birch Lake Reservoir), effective **2026-11-10**, for failing performance and production requirements. It "is not located within the area that Twin Metals has previously sought to mine." Friends of the BW lists other lease cancellation windows in 2035, 2036, 2037 and 2043. — [WDIO](https://www.wdio.com/top-stories/minnesota-dnr-cancels-one-of-twin-metals-mineral-leases/); [MinnPost, 2026-09-15](https://www.minnpost.com/environment/2026/09/walz-actions-setting-precedents-for-next-governor-in-boundary-waters-fight/)
- Twin Metals says it has spent more than 16 years and over $600–650 million on the project. — [WDIO, 2026-08-18](https://www.wdio.com/top-stories/ely-community-reacts-to-governor-walzs-new-executive-order/); [Circle of Blue](https://www.circleofblue.org/2026/great-lakes/senate-vote-tests-future-of-boundary-waters-protections/)

### Inferences
- As of 2026-11-03 no mine is permitted. The federal withdrawal is gone by statute. The federal leases are still cancelled and in litigation, with no reissuance found. A state executive order blocks state permits. The next governor (Klobuchar-D or Demuth-R, chosen 2026-11-03) decides whether that order stands.

## 5. PolyMet / NorthMet / NewRange (Babbitt–Hoyt Lakes, Lake Superior side)

### Takeaway
PolyMet proposed an open-pit mine in 2005 at the old LTV taconite site between Babbitt and Hoyt Lakes, which drains to Lake Superior via the St. Louis River. It went through a long EIS (2005–2016) and got permits in 2018–19. Courts and agencies then knocked those back (2020–2023). Glencore took control, and a 2023 joint venture with Teck renamed the project NewRange. In 2026 it filed a redesign. The DNR ordered a Supplemental EIS (Oct 2026), and the Army Corps is taking comment on a new wetlands permit.

### Cited Findings
- NorthMet site: at and around a long-abandoned taconite plant between Hoyt Lakes and Babbitt, "in the Lake Superior basin." Proposal submitted Feb. 2005. The 2009 draft EIS was rated "Unsatisfactory – Inadequate" (EU-3) by the EPA (2010-02-18). Supplemental draft EIS December 2013 (EPA EC-2). Final EIS June 2015. DNR adequacy decision 2016-03-03. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- DNR permits 2018-11-01; MPCA air and water permits December 2018; Army Corps 2019-03-19. Court of Appeals reversed three DNR permits in January 2020. MN Supreme Court affirmed in April 2021 and remanded for a contested case. In June 2023 the Army Corps revoked the wetland permit. In August 2023 the MN Supreme Court suspended the water permit. In November 2023 an ALJ recommended denying the permit to mine because the design posed too great a contamination risk. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel)
- The Fond du Lac Band is "a resolute opponent." Polymet was "a subsidiary of Glencore." The Teck joint venture in 2023 renamed it NewRange. Three open pits, the deepest about 700 ft. Ore about 0.79% copper. Up to 500 jobs claimed. 912.5 acres of wetlands affected. Drainage via the Partridge, Embarrass and St. Louis rivers to Lake Superior. — [Wikipedia: PolyMet Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolyMet_Mine)
- 2026-07-29: NewRange notified the DNR of design changes. On about 2026-10-08 the DNR ruled a state-only **Supplemental EIS** is required, after input from the Grand Portage and Fond du Lac bands. The project is "about 6 miles north of Hoyt Lakes." — [WDIO, 2026-10-08](https://www.wdio.com/mining-news/dnr-says-they-will-do-a-supplemental-eis-for-newrange-copper-nickels-project/)
- September 2026: the Army Corps extended comment on a new NewRange permit application. — [DVIDS, 2026-09-08](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/574257/corps-engineers-extends-comment-period-northmet-mining-project)
- 2026-10-19: NewRange filed an exploration plan for up to 11 drill sites 4.5 mi south of Babbitt, in the St. Louis River watershed. — [KQDS/Fox21](https://www.fox21online.com/news/local/mining-exploration-plan-near-babbitt-submitted-by-newrange/article_5fd046d6-4441-4843-9af4-6b103718a269.html)
- Former DNR commissioner Tom Landwehr helped approve the Babbitt project in 2018 but opposes Twin Metals: "They are two completely different sites... 'Not this mine, not this site, not now, not ever.'" — [Wikipedia: Twin Metals mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- Klobuchar (2026-08-18) backed letting reviews proceed for "other proposed mining projects that don't endanger the Boundary Waters." — [MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/walz-executive-order-block-copper-mining-near-boundary-waters)
- A third project, Talon Metals' Tamarack nickel project near McGregor/Tamarack (Rio Tinto JV), is opposed by the Mille Lacs Band. — [LRL guide](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/guides/guides?issue=coppernickel); [MinnPost, 2026-09-14](https://www.minnpost.com/environment/2026/09/far-from-the-boundary-waters-the-mille-lacs-tribe-resists-another-copper-nickel-mine/)

### Inferences
- The watershed split matters in the story. Babbitt/Hoyt Lakes drains south to Lake Superior; Birch Lake/Kawishiwi drains north into the BWCA. Many moderates (Landwehr, Klobuchar, USW) accept the first and not the second.

## 6. Local sentiment: Ely and the Range, outfitters, unions, tribes, politicians

### Takeaway
Ely is split down the middle, and the split often runs between old mining families and newer arrivals or tourism businesses. But there are cross-cutters: a retired miner against, a Bois Forte member's son for. Statewide polls run about 60–67% against a mine near the BWCA. Northern Minnesota is less opposed (56% against, 35% for in Oct 2026, up from 22% support in 2020). Labor and Range politicians of both parties (DFL Sen. Grant Hauschild, Republican Rep. Roger Skraba, Republican Rep. Pete Stauber, USW) objected to Walz's order. Conservation groups, many outfitters and the Ojibwe bands opposed the mine.

## 7. The science and economics each side argues

### Takeaway
Opponents stress sulfide ore turning into acid mine drainage, the site's location upstream of the BWCA (Birch Lake → Kawishiwi → wilderness → Voyageurs → Hudson Bay), dry-stack tailings in a wet climate, and boom-bust economics. Supporters stress jobs, school trust revenue, domestic critical minerals (copper, nickel, cobalt, PGMs) for batteries and defense, modern engineering such as dry stack, underground mining and backfill, and the right to a full review. Neutral experts question whether the economics and refining capacity add up.

### Cited Findings
- Tidwell (Forest Service, 2016): "acid drainage can be created in all phases of mining, from construction to storing waste rock and tailings." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- Copper-nickel mining "carries with it much more serious risks for serious water pollution than iron ore mining." The withdrawal area matters because pollution "could flow directly into the protected wilderness area." — [MPR](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/walz-executive-order-block-copper-mining-near-boundary-waters)
- Sen. Martin Heinrich: "this type of copper mining has never been done without polluting the water." — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/16/minnesota-boundary-waters-mining-congress.html)
- The DNR has noted that wet dry-stack material "can leach heavy metals" and can create toxic dust. — [Wikipedia (citing MinnPost)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
- The waters flow into Voyageurs National Park "and, eventually, the Arctic Ocean" (via Hudson Bay). — [CBS/WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/boundary-water-mining-debate/)
- Twin Metals says Minnesota's rules require projects to "prove they can meet some of the world's most rigorous environmental and labor standards." — [CBS/WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/boundary-water-mining-debate/). The company claims its deposit holds 99% of US nickel reserves, 88% of cobalt and a third of copper. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine) (company claim, unverified)
- School trust: the cancelled state lease is school trust land "intended to maximize revenue generation." A school trust amendment is on the 2026-11-03 ballot. — [MinnPost, 2026-09-15](https://www.minnpost.com/environment/2026/09/walz-actions-setting-precedents-for-next-governor-in-boundary-waters-fight/)

- MPR, on permitting: regulators are "not deciding on the merits of the project." Permits turn on meeting numeric standards. — [Wikipedia (quoting MPR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Metals_mine)
