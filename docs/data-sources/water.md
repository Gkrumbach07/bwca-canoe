## The top 3 for water

1. **NWI wetlands (MN DNR, the owner's link).** **Via the bake:** a new `wetlands.mjs` fetches the 120,836 polygons by REST once and caches them for the bake, a few hundred MB raw. The bake then writes one wetland class per 10 m cell into the tiles: open bog, conifer bog, shrub, marsh, hardwood swamp, in-lake emergent, aquatic bed. That replaces the noise bogs and meadows in fields.js. Placement then puts pads and reeds only on AB and EM2. The class adds one byte channel to the tiles, mostly zeros, so it compresses to a few MB across the release.
2. **LakeFinder (MN DNR), joined by DOW with ice-out and wild rice acres.** **Via a runtime table:** for each lake, its max depth, species list (with catch per net), stocking, littoral acres, median ice-out and rice acres. Make it with a one-off script like site-lakes.mjs, write it to something like `src/lake-facts.json`, and key it to the bake's lake ids. 907 lakes come to ~100–150 KB. It gives the game real fish per lake, and a real depth to scale the bowl of the 670 lakes that have no contours.
3. **Rapids and waterfalls from NHD (the owner's other link), with names from OSM.** **Via a runtime table** like world-fixes.json: 122 NHD features and 71 OSM ones, with snapped lines across the river, kind (rapids or falls) and name, ~15 KB. It marks where water-grade's steps are real white water or a real falls, and names them on the map (Curtain, Rebecca, Basswood, Kawishiwi Falls…). NHD's 3,151 above-water rocks can ride along as in-lake boulders, ~40 KB.

**Next after these:** UMN LakeBrowser clarity and CDOM (1,293 lakes; the bake, as a third source in clarity.mjs; a few MB cached, a byte or two per lake shipped), which gives every lake its own water colour. Also the USGS Kawishiwi gauge's 12 months of water temperature and flow, under 1 KB, a constant table that sets the swim cold and the spring rapids.

## Sensitive data: say no

- **Loon nest sites.** The loon program publishes no nests, and BWCA nest surveys by USFS or NPS researchers aren't public. Use the per-acre density rule only. Never place a "nest" at a real spot.
- **Wild rice and the tribal monitoring data.** Manoomin beds matter to the Bois Forte, Grand Portage and Fond du Lac Ojibwe, and are harvested under the 1854 Treaty. Use the DNR and MPCA lake lists and NWI marsh polygons, at the scale of a lake. Don't seek the Collaborative State-Tribal Wild Rice Monitoring observation points that the 2023 model was trained on. Don't present any band's harvest areas.
- **MnModel.** The wild rice points are harmless, but the rest of MnDOT's MnModel package is an archaeological site-prediction model, and its site layers are restricted. Don't use them.
- **Rare species in the water** (Natural Heritage Information System: rare mussels, plants, fish), and **colonial waterbird nesting sites** (heron, gull and tern colonies). Both are restricted. Lakes of Biological Significance gives only a rank per lake, which is fine; never the locations behind it.

## Gotchas for whoever builds this

- `gisdata.mn.gov`'s CKAN API is gone: every URL 301s to gis.data.mn.gov. Downloads are now `operations.gis.data.mn.gov/api/publicdownload/download/<n>/<file>`. Find the link in the item description at `arcgis.com/sharing/rest/content/items/<id>?f=json`.
- LakeFinder's old `lakefinder_json.cgi` is gone. Use `maps.dnr.state.mn.us/cgi-bin/lakefinder/search.cgi` (lat, lon and radius in m; no cap seen at 159 results). `fishSpecies` is one comma-joined string inside an array.
- WebSearch failed (a model error) and Overpass timed out. Instead, osmium over the game's cached Minnesota PBF answered the OSM questions in seconds.
