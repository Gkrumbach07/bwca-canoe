# Theme C: land (elevation, rock, soil, imagery, land cover, glacial features)

Checked 2026-10-26 against the bake box `bwca` in scripts/config.mjs (S 47.78, W −92.55, N 48.4, E −89.88). Every "count" below is a live `returnCountOnly` (or SDA/STAC/TNM) query over that box, unless it names a smaller box. "Lake One box" is `AREAS.lakeone`.

**The finding that matters most:** the game's 10 m 3DEP ramps are a data problem, not a shading problem. Minnesota flew the whole US side of the BWCA in spring 2021 (leaf-off, QL1, 0.35 m pulse spacing), and the state now serves it as cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs that can be read by window over HTTP. Over the Lake One box (12.5 × 8.5 km), I measured:

| Source | Max slope | Area over 35° | Area over 45° |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3DEP at 10 m | 40.6° | 0.003 % | 0 |
| LiDAR resampled to 10 m | 44.7° | 0.005 % | 0 |
| LiDAR at 2 m | 72.9° | 0.57 % | 0.135 % |

The 3DEP figure matches site.js's "steepest 10 m cells are 32–40°". At 2 m, 9,113 of the 10 m cells (0.86 %) hold a pixel steeper than 45°, and 1,684 hold one steeper than 55°. Those are the real bluffs. Pulling that window at 2 m took 6 s.

## Top 3 for land

1. **MnTOPO 2nd-gen LiDAR DEM (1–2 m), in the bake.** In scripts/elevation.mjs, read COG windows of `dem2m_{5,6,7}_{52,53}_m.tif` with geotiff.js over HTTP for the US side.
   - Average to the 10 m grid for heights.
   - Add a byte per cell for the max 2 m slope, and a byte for 2 m relief or roughness. site.js then finds cliffs, talus and boulder fields from data instead of from the drawn-mesh step.
   - Take lake levels from the hydro-flattened water.
   - Terrarium stays for Ontario and the far field; blend at the border.
   - Download: roughly 3–5 GB of 2 m windows for the box, cached in data-cache. Output: about 138 M cells × 2 bytes, roughly 30–60 MB more gzipped across all tiles.
2. **MGS bedrock geology D-05 + outcrop database D-02.**
   - Rock unit becomes a small runtime table: an 80 m rock-unit raster of about 1–2 MB, plus a ~20-entry palette and cliff style (granite/tonalite, migmatite, greenstone, Knife Lake slate, biotite schist, Duluth anorthosite/troctolite, Logan diabase, Rove/Puckwunge). Cliffs and shore rock get the right colour and shape without re-baking.
   - The outcrop polygons (99,666) go into the bake as a "mapped bare rock" bit on the 10 m grid. Pull is about 50–100 MB of GeoJSON.
3. **DNR Potentially Wet Histosols + SSURGO, in the bake.** Rasterise into one "ground kind" byte per 10 m cell, replacing the noise in fields.js (meadowAt/sandAt/knobAt):
   - bog kinds from wet histosols (coniferous bog, open bog, shrub wetland, marsh)
   - rock-outcrop %, bouldery/rubbly and organic from SSURGO map units

   Pull is about 100k + 69k polygons, ~150 MB of GeoJSON. Output: 1 byte per cell, about 15–25 MB gzipped.

## Sensitive data

- **MnModel (MnDOT).** It is Minnesota's archaeological site-predictive model. Its site-potential and "From Everywhere To Everywhere" (historic pedestrian travel) layers point toward where archaeological sites are likely, so **no**. Its geology and wetness inputs are harmless, but we don't need them either.
- **MGS D-03 depth to bedrock.** It leaves out certain Tribal Nations' lands at their request (Grand Portage lies at the box's east edge). **No** to backfilling or publishing a substitute there.
- **Minnesota Biological Survey rare-species records.** These sit behind the NPC polygons. They weren't public in what I found and must stay out. NPC community polygons themselves are fine.
- **Nothing else.** No pictograph, archaeological or nest locations turned up in the land datasets.
